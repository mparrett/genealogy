**What works well:**

The narrative arc is strong — arrival, settlement, war service, marriage, hardship, death, and the scattering of orphaned children. That closing paragraph lands well as a summary without being sentimental. You also do a good job contextualizing details that would otherwise be inert: explaining *why* a miller mattered on the Ozark frontier, or why a Missourian might enlist in New York in 1865. Those asides show you're writing for a reader, not just logging facts.

The sourcing is impressively granular. Footnoting the delinquent tax notice, the guardian appointment, the estate settlement — that level of documentation gives the whole piece credibility and makes it useful as a reference for anyone else researching this line.

**Structural suggestions:**

The paragraph starting "Frederick owned 100 acres…" is doing a lot of heavy lifting. It covers land ownership, Sarah's death, the tax delinquency, the 1876 census, the Texas County question, *and* sets up Frederick's own death. I'd consider splitting it after "strain on the household after Sarah's death," letting the census and geographic ambiguity start a new paragraph. The paragraph about children and the 1880s county history is a bit tangled. You introduce George and Charles Fred, then pivot to a cautionary note about a secondary source attributing six children to "a Kuthe household," then circle back to George.
