## The answer

**Bridget Gleeson, resident at Scagh, daughter of James Gleeson, farmer**, married **Thomas O'Shea of Croom, farmer, son of Thomas Shea, farmer**, at the Roman Catholic chapel of Croom on **27 August 1864**.

Both of the discriminators the plan was built around are present, and both point the same way:

- **Her father is James Gleeson** — the name of Patrick Gleeson's father.
- **Her residence is Scagh** — Patrick Gleeson's own townland, the same townland written into Catherine's baptism two years earlier.

### Corrections this settles

- **The IrishGenealogy index date of 24 August 1864 is wrong.** The certificate itself reads *twenty-seventh of August*, agreeing with the Croom Catholic register and with FamilySearch's independent index of the same GRO return. Use **27 August 1864**.
- **The witness is William Storan**, confirming the reading taken from the parish register and correcting `facts.md` ("Gulielmo Strau") and `assets/external/nli-croom/153.md` ("Gulielmo Stran").

## Q2.1 — The 1862 baptism entry, re-read at image level

**Source:** NLI Croom CPR, microfilm 02427/07, frame **81**, first entry on the left page.

> **Januarii 31. Catharina filia Patritii Gleeson & Mar[garitae Moloney] de Scagh — Suscep[tores] Johanne Moloney & Brig. Gleeson.**

| Field | As written |
|---|---|
| Date | 31 January 1862 |
| Child | Catharina |
| Father | Patritii Gleeson |
| Mother | Mar[garitae] Moloney |
| Residence | **de Scagh** |
| Sponsor 1 | **Johanne Moloney** |
| Sponsor 2 | **Brig. Gleeson** |

The surname is unmistakably Gleeson — the letterforms match the father's name three words earlier in the same line and pen. The forename is **abbreviated in the register as "Brig."**, not written out; Brigida is the obvious expansion but the record does not spell it. The role is not misread: *Susceptores* introduces both sponsors, one from each side of the marriage — a Moloney for the mother, a Gleeson for the father.

**Townland spelling.** The parish register writes **Scagh** in 1862; the 1864 civil marriage writes **Scagh**; the 1875 civil birth writes **Skagh**; the 1864 Petty Sessions record writes **Scaugh**. Same place, four spellings, none to be corrected to the others.

## Applying the plan's Q2 decision rule

The rule's first branch: *"Matching father or residence plus compatible associates: treat the sponsor and bride as **probably the same woman**."*

We have **father and residence and associates** — all three. The rule is satisfied with room to spare.

**Recommended wording for the sponsor identification: the 1862 godmother and the 1864 bride are the same woman — probable, bordering on established.** The only reason to stop short of flat "established" is that the baptism gives the sponsor as an abbreviation, "Brig. Gleeson", with no residence of her own; the identification runs through the townland of the family she was standing for.

### On "sister" — much stronger, still an inference

Bridget was living at **Scagh** in August 1864 and her father was **James Gleeson, farmer**. Patrick Gleeson lived at Scagh, and Patrick's father was **James Gleeson**. Three readings survive:

1. **Bridget was Patrick's sister, living in her married brother's household at Scagh before her own marriage.** This is the natural reading, it is an extremely common arrangement, and it explains neatly why she was chosen as godmother to Patrick's firstborn in 1862 — a resident aunt is the obvious choice.
2. James Gleeson himself had moved from Hospital parish to Scagh by 1864. Nothing yet places him there.
3. A different James Gleeson family was resident at Scagh. Possible — the name is common — but it requires two unrelated Gleeson households in one small townland, one of which happens to supply a godmother to the other.

Reading 1 is much the best supported. **But no document yet states the relationship**, so "sister" remains an inference from a shared father's forename plus a shared townland, not a recorded fact.

**The document that would close it:** a baptism for **Bridget Gleeson, daughter of James Gleeson and Honora O'Brien, Hospital parish, c. 1839–1842**. Her stated age of 22 in August 1864 implies birth around 1841–42. Note the tension worth watching: `facts.md` already documents a sister **Margaret Gleeson baptized Nov/Dec 1842** at Herbertstown, which leaves very little room for a Bridget born in 1842. Either Bridget's age is a year or two light — routine on Irish certificates — or she was born c. 1839–40. Search the Hospital register across 1838–1843 rather than anchoring on 1842.

## Incidental

Ten days after Bridget's marriage, on **6 September 1864**, the Croom register records *Gulielmus Riordan et Maria Hanly … **Jacobo Gleeson** et Catherina Barden sponsoribus*. `facts.md` flags this James Gleeson as plausibly Patrick's older brother. Given that Bridget's father was also a James Gleeson, the Croom "Jacobo Gleeson" of 1864 is worth re-examining as potentially either the brother or the father.
