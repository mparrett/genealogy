## Significance

- **Independently verified** via NARA M596 quarterly abstracts on Ancestry
- Confirms **Andreas Kuthe** (b. c. 1798) as a real person, not just family tradition
- Shows Frederick arrived as a **child age 7** in 1834 with his family, not as an adult in 1845
- Reveals previously unknown family members: wife **Anna D.** (b. c. 1800) and daughter **Margareth** (b. c. 1831)
- A family group (parents + two children) suggests a permanent family emigration, not a solo economic migration
- Origin listed as **Germany** — exact locality within Hesse unconfirmed; family tradition says Darmstadt, possibly related Küthe family from Vöhl
- No vessel name recorded — consistent with the quarterly abstract format (M596), which often omits ship details per NARA documentation

## Conflicts with Existing Research

### 1845 Olbers Manifest
The Olbers passenger list (Bremen to New Orleans, June 1845) lists a Küthe family headed by Heinrich Küthe (52) from Vöhl, Waldeck, with an 18-year-old "Friederich." Since our Frederick was already in the US by 1834, the Olbers Friederich is a different person. The two families may be related (overlapping names suggest kinship) but this is unconfirmed. See `research/analysis/kuthe-two-fredericks.md`.

### Birth Year
- This manifest: age 7 in 1834 → born c. 1827
- 1880 census: "about 57" → born c. 1823
- Death in 1900 "at approximately 77" → born c. 1823
- Range of c. 1822–1827 is plausible given imprecise 19th-century ages

### Probable Vessel: Weisencorn

On the M596 quarterly abstract (page 67), the passenger **"Jno M. Wiesencorn"** (age 54, male, Germany) appears just a few entries below the Kuth family. The ship log shows a vessel named **Weisencorn** arriving May 25, 1834 carrying 124 German passengers. If passengers on the quarterly abstract are grouped by vessel (which is typical for these abstracts), the proximity of the Kuths to Wiesencorn suggests they traveled on the same ship. The passenger may be the ship's owner, captain, or namesake.

This identification is **probable but not confirmed** — it requires cross-referencing the original manifest (M255) or finding another passenger from the same abstract page in a ship-specific index.

### Wilhelm Kuthe and the Hesse Connection

Wilhelm Kuthe (1842, Melsungen) is noteworthy: same surname, same port, same era, same region of Germany, and nearly the same age as Andreas. Both were bakers/tradesmen from Hesse arriving in Baltimore within a decade of each other. Melsungen, Vöhl, and the Darmstadt area are all within Hesse, suggesting a broader Kuthe kinship network in the region. Whether Wilhelm is connected to Andreas (or to Heinrich of the Olbers) remains unconfirmed.
