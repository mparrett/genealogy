## Who she was

**Catherine Gleeson**, baptised at Croom on **31 January 1862** — the eldest child of Patrick Gleeson and Margaret Moloney of Scagh. Her baptism is image-verified: *"Catharina filia Patritii Gleeson & Mar[garitae Moloney] de Scagh — Suscep. Johanne Moloney & Brig. Gleeson."* She was about 22 months older than James.

On **29 November 1879**, aged seventeen, she married **John Hayes** at Croom — Rev. John Quinlan officiating, witnesses Michael and Catherine Shanahan. John was a local Croom-area man from an active Hayes network in the parish, not an outsider.

Then a long gap. The 1920 census gives her immigration year as **1895** — so she and John apparently stayed in Ireland about sixteen years after marrying before crossing.

## Her life in New York

- **1920, Egbert Street, Staten Island.** John Hayes, 55, head; Catherine, 48, wife; and one boarder — **John P. Leo, 29**.
- **No surviving children**, confirmed across the 1900 through 1940 censuses.
- **John died in 1929**, leaving her widowed for fifteen years.
- By 1930 and 1940 she was living with **Thomas and Helen Ryan** — Helen being her maternal first cousin, born Ellen Kelly at Ballymackeamore, Adare, to John Kelly and Kate Moloney. Close-kin care of an aging widow.
- **Died 1 November 1944 at 64 Edison Street, Staten Island.** Helen Ryan was informant and executor. Buried in the shared Ryan plot at St Mary's Cemetery, Grasmere, under the anglicised spelling **GLEASON**. Also in that plot: **Nora Gleason, 1881–1939** — very likely her sister Bridget, born Croom 11 Oct 1881, who went by Nora in America.

**That boarder is worth a second look.** **Leo** is a Croom surname — it turns up twice in the marriage register sweep I ran earlier, including the Leo–Donovan wedding of 18 October 1875 where the witnesses were Thomas Moloney and Margaret Gleeson. A John P. Leo boarding with the Hayeses on Staten Island in 1920 looks like the Croom network reassembling itself in New York, and nobody has traced him.

## Her age is a mess, and now resolvable

Four different birth years attach to her:

| Source | Implied birth |
|---|---|
| **Croom baptism, image-verified** | **late January 1862** |
| Grasmere headstone | 1865 |
| 1920 census (age 48) | ~1872 |
| NY death certificate (age 68) | 14 February 1876 |

The baptism is the only primary record of the event itself; the rest are self-reported or third-hand. Against 1862, her death certificate understates her age by **fourteen years** — extreme, but her mother Margaret shows the same habit at nine to ten years, so it's a family pattern rather than an anomaly.

**And that dissolves the coincidence your vetting plan was built around.** Q3 flagged that Nora's certificate says 10 January 1876 while Catherine's says 14 February 1876 — two sisters apparently born five weeks apart. With Catherine's real birth established as January 1862, her "1876" is simply wrong and the coincidence evaporates. That question is closed, and not by the route the plan anticipated.
