# Griffith's Valuation — testing the Hospital → Croom kin corridor

**Run:** 2026-08-20, askaboutireland.ie (Griffith's Valuation, Co. Limerick ~1851, Co. Tipperary ~1850–52)
**Hypothesis under test:** `research/analysis/gleeson-moloney-consanguinity-1860.md` §5–6
**Method:** name search by civil parish, townland read from each row's map link. All Co. Limerick Gleeson records (330 rows) were swept and aggregated; targeted parish queries for the rest.

---

## The prediction

The 1860 dispensation makes Patrick and Margaret **second cousins once removed**, sharing an ancestral couple born c. 1710–1750. For that to be possible, **either** a Gleeson line already reached into the Croom / Croagh / Kilfinny area, **or** a Molony/Kenny line already reached into Hospital / Emly. Griffith's (~1851) falls **after** Patrick left Hospital and **before** he surfaces at Scagh in 1859 — precisely the right moment to look.

---

## ⭐ Result: CONFIRMED, and in one direction only

### 1. There was a Gleeson at Skagh before Patrick got there

| Croom civil parish, ~1851 | Townland |
|---|---|
| John Gleeson | Croom |
| Michael Gleeson | Dunnaman |
| **Timothy Gleeson** | **SKAGH** |

**Patrick's own townland.** He is first attested at "Sean"/Scaugh in September 1859; a Timothy Gleeson was holding land there roughly eight years earlier. Patrick did not move to a random townland twenty-five miles from home — **he moved to a townland that already had a Gleeson in it.**

### 2. The Boherygeela cluster — and Stephen was already there

**Boherygeela** (spanning Tullabracky and Glenogra civil parishes) held, in ~1851:

> **Stephen Gleeson · Anne Gleeson · John Gleeson · William Gleeson** — alongside **John Moloney**, Patrick Quare, Mary Howard, Purcell, John Kinealy, Mary Barry, Simon Riordan

`facts.md` had Stephen Gleeson — the 1829 Hospital baptism sponsor and probable paternal uncle — as having "later migrated to Croom area himself and settled at Boherygeela," inferred from his 1862 Croom marriage. **Griffith's confirms he was at Boherygeela by ~1851** — a decade before that marriage and eight years before Patrick arrived. Also at Crean and Grange: more John and Stephen Gleesons.

**Anne Gleeson** at Boherygeela is worth noting separately: an *Anna Gleeson* witnesses the marriage of Jacobus Shanahan at Croom in June 1869.

### 3. Gleesons and a Moloney were neighbours in Margaret's own parish

| Croagh civil parish, ~1851 — townland **LISNAMUCK** |
|---|
| **James Gleeson** · **Michael Gleeson** · **John Moloney** · Michael Browne · Robert Pigott · Thomas Connors · Ellen Frawley · Timothy Euright |

Croagh is Margaret's baptismal parish. **Gleesons and a Moloney holding land in the same townland there in 1851** is the single most suggestive result of the run — and it is echoed at Boherygeela, where a Gleeson cluster and a John Moloney also share a townland.

Two townlands, twenty miles apart, each with Gleesons and Moloneys as neighbours, one of them in the bride's own parish.

---

## The reverse direction is NOT supported

Sweeping Molony/Moloney and Kenny across Patrick's home district (Hospital, Emly, Knockainy, Kilcullane, Ballinard, Athneasy, Kilfrush, Kilteely, Grean, Knocklong, Galbally):

| Surname | Total in Co. Limerick | In Patrick's home district |
|---|---|---|
| Moloney | 139 | **1** — Michael Moloney, Kilcullane |
| Kenny | 95 | **3** — William (Athneasy), Patrick (Hospital), Margaret (Knockainy) |

Against a Gleeson presence in the Croom cluster running to dozens of households, this is negligible.

**Conclusion: the corridor ran Gleeson-westward.** The pre-existing link between the two districts was a Gleeson one, not a Moloney one. Whatever the shared ancestry is, the Gleesons were the mobile party.

## Other findings, including negatives

### ⭐ Malachy Gleeson at Herbertstown — and a parish correction

> **Kilcullane civil parish, townland HERBERTSTOWN (O'GRADY): Malachy Gleeson · John Gleeson**

Two things here.

**(a) Herbertstown is in KILCULLANE civil parish**, not Hospital. `facts.md` files the 1838, 1839 and 1842 Herbertstown baptisms under "Hospital parish" — correct as to the *Catholic* parish, but the *civil* parish is Kilcullane. Worth recording, because every land-record search keys on the civil parish.

**(b) Malachy is a rare forename and it is a lead.** Patrick's newly-found brother **Malachy** was baptised 12 July 1835 — far too young to be a Griffith's occupier in 1851. So the Malachy Gleeson holding land at Herbertstown is an **older man of the same name**, in the family's own townland. `facts.md` already notes "a Malachy Gleeson with wife Biddy Dineen is raising children at Hospital from 1841." Under Irish naming custom a son called Malachy in 1835 points to a Malachy in the parental generation. **This is a strong candidate for a paternal uncle or great-uncle** and a much better handle than any James.

### ⭐ Baggotstown West is real — five Gleesons

> **Knockainy civil parish, BAGGOTSTOWN WEST: John · James · Patrick · Denis · William Gleeson**
> (also Knockainy: Catherine at Bottomstown, Laurence at Gortacloona, Patrick at Rathanny)

The vetting plan's **Q1.3 "Baggotstown West two brothers" lead** has been sitting untested. It is now concrete: five Gleeson households in one townland, in the civil parish adjoining the family's own. Q1.3 should be promoted.

### ❌ Patrick's father held no land

> **Hospital civil parish: Michael (Ballycahill) · Patrick (Barrysfarm) · Michael (Castlefarm) · Michael (Newtown) — NO JAMES**

**James Gleeson, Patrick's father, is not a Griffith's occupier in Hospital parish.** He was either landless — a labourer or an unrecorded sub-tenant — or dead by ~1851. His last documented child is Catherine, baptised 10 March 1850, so he was alive at least until mid-1849. This is a **material finding about the family's economic position**: they were baptising children across three townlands but holding no valued tenancy in the parish under the father's name.

### ❌ No Gleesons at all in Emly parish, Co. Tipperary

Neither at Ballinvreena nor anywhere else in Emly civil parish. Consistent with — and reinforcing — the earlier finding that Gleesons were rare at Emly (four baptisms indexed 1820–45) and that the family had left Ballinvreena for Herbertstown by 1838. **They held no land at Ballinvreena in 1851.**

This weakens, without killing, the *Baile an Bhrianaigh* hypothesis: an **Edmond Brien** holds at Emly townland, but **no Brien or O'Brien appears at Ballinvreena**. The Tithe Applotment (~1826–34) is still the right test, since it catches the family while they were actually there.

### ❌ Kilfinny is empty of both surnames

No Gleesons and no Moloneys in Kilfinny civil parish. This **corroborates the 2026-06-09 reconciliation**, which found that Griffith's shows no Molony at Ballymackeamore and concluded that the Molony and Kelly families there were post-1851 in-migrants.

---

## ⚠️ What this does and does not establish

**It does establish** that Patrick moved into an area with a substantial, pre-existing Gleeson population — including one in his own destination townland and a cluster containing his probable uncle — rather than into a Gleeson-free void. The prediction made in §5–6 of the consanguinity analysis is **confirmed at the level at which it was made**.

**It does not establish kinship.** Gleeson is among the commoner surnames of east Limerick and Tipperary. Co-presence is **necessary but not sufficient**: none of these people is yet linked to Patrick by any document. The Skagh and Boherygeela results are suggestive because of *where* they are, not because the names prove anything on their own.

**The honest summary:** the corridor hypothesis survived a test it could have failed, and it failed the reverse test cleanly enough to tell us which direction the link ran.

4. **Baggotstown West (Q1.3)** — now a concrete five-household target.
5. **Tithe Applotment, Ballinvreena** (~1826–34) — the family were resident in 1833, so this is the record that should catch them, and it will settle the O'Brien-ground question.
