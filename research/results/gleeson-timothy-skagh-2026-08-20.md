## ⭐ Finding 1 — the Storin connection

**Timothy Gleeson married ELLEN STORIN at Croom on 3 March 1867.**

**Storan/Storin is already in this family's file.** The 1864 civil marriage of **Bridget Gleeson and Thomas O'Shea** — Bridget being the 1862 godmother to Patrick's firstborn, and a Gleeson kinswoman of unestablished degree — was witnessed by **William Storan**. The conclusions memo of 2026-08-18 lists "trace the Storan/Storin family" as an open lead precisely because that surname appeared from nowhere.

So the Skagh landholder married into the same small family that stood witness at a Gleeson wedding three years earlier. That is **two independent Gleeson–Storan ties inside three years**, in a parish where the surname is otherwise rare.

⚠️ This is suggestive, not probative. It raises the prior that Timothy belongs to the same Gleeson network as Patrick and Bridget; it does not establish it.

## ⭐ Finding 2 — the 1876 death fits the Griffith's occupier exactly

**Timothy Gleeson, died 1876, Croom, aged 72** → born about **1804**.

A man born in 1804 would be about **47 in 1851** — precisely the profile of a Griffith's head-of-household. This is the strongest candidate for **the Timothy who held Skagh**.

That produces a clean two-Timothy reading:

- **Timothy senior**, b. ~1804, the Skagh occupier in Griffith's, d. 1876 aged 72.
- **A younger Timothy**, b. ~1837 — represented by the 1894 (age 57) and 1905 (age 68) deaths, which cannot both be the same man but do bracket the same birth year. Plausibly a son, and plausibly the 1867 groom, who would have been about 30.

⚠️ **Unresolved: which Timothy married Ellen Storin in 1867.** Senior would have been 63 — possible as a remarriage, not typical. The certificate settles it, because it gives the groom's age *and* his father's name.

**The 1867 certificate is the decisive document**, and it is the one still unread. Column 8 will name **Timothy's father**. If that name is **James**, it puts Timothy in the same generation-and-family frame as Patrick and is a major result. If it is something else, it still names a new Gleeson of the parental generation at Skagh.
