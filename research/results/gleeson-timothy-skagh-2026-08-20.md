## ⭐ Finding 1 — the Storin connection

**Timothy Gleeson married ELLEN STORIN at Croom on 3 March 1867.**

**Storan/Storin is already in this family's file.** The 1864 civil marriage of **Bridget Gleeson and Thomas O'Shea** — Bridget being the 1862 godmother to Patrick's firstborn, and a Gleeson kinswoman of unestablished degree — was witnessed by **William Storan**. The conclusions memo of 2026-08-18 lists "trace the Storan/Storin family" as an open lead precisely because that surname appeared from nowhere.

So the Skagh landholder married into the same small family that stood witness at a Gleeson wedding three years earlier. That is **two independent Gleeson–Storan ties inside three years**, in a parish where the surname is otherwise rare.

⚠️ This is suggestive, not probative. It raises the prior that Timothy belongs to the same Gleeson network as Patrick and Bridget; it does not establish it.

## ⭐ Finding 2 — the 1876 death fits the Griffith's occupier exactly

**Timothy Gleeson, died 1876, Croom, aged 72** → born about **1804**.

A man born in 1804 would be about **47 in 1851** — precisely the profile of a Griffith's head-of-household. This is the strongest candidate for **the Timothy who held Skagh**.

That produces a clean two-Timothy reading:

- **Timothy senior**, b. ~1804, the Skagh occupier in Griffith's, d. 1876 aged 72.
- **A younger Timothy**, b. ~1837 — represented by the 1894 (age 57) and 1905 (age 68) deaths, which cannot both be the same man but do bracket the same birth year. Plausibly a son, and plausibly the 1867 groom, who would have been about 30.

⚠️ **Unresolved: which Timothy married Ellen Storin in 1867.** Senior would have been 63 — possible as a remarriage, not typical. The certificate settles it, because it gives the groom's age *and* his father's name.

**The 1867 certificate is the decisive document**, and it is the one still unread. Column 8 will name **Timothy's father**. If that name is **James**, it puts Timothy in the same generation-and-family frame as Patrick and is a major result. If it is something else, it still names a new Gleeson of the parental generation at Skagh.

3. **His father was JAMES GLEESON, a farmer.**

## 4. ⭐⭐ This probably explains why the Bridget search failed

The August sweeps hunted **Bridget Gleeson** — the 1862 godmother to Patrick's firstborn, who married Thomas O'Shea on 27 August 1864 — through the **Hospital** register (Jan 1836 – Dec 1844) and the **Emly** register, on the assumption that "daughter of James Gleeson, farmer" on her 1864 certificate meant *Patrick's* father James. **Nothing was found**, and the conclusion recorded was that she was "a Gleeson kinswoman of unestablished degree."

Now look at the two certificates side by side:

| | **Bridget Gleeson**, m. 27 Aug 1864 | **Timothy Gleeson**, m. 3 Mar 1867 |
|---|---|---|
| Residence | **Scagh** | **Scagh** |
| Father | **James Gleeson, farmer** | **James Gleeson, farmer** |
| Witness at the other's wedding | — | **Bridget Gleeson** ✓ |
| Witness at hers | William **Storan** | married Ellen **Storin** |

**Hypothesis [T2]: Bridget and Timothy were siblings — children of a James Gleeson of Scagh.**

Same townland, same father's name and occupation, married under the same priest three years apart, with Bridget standing witness at Timothy's wedding, and a Storan/Storin on both sides.

**If that is right, the Hospital and Emly searches were looking in the wrong county.** Bridget was not a daughter of James Gleeson and Honora O'Brien of Hospital at all — she was the daughter of a **different James Gleeson, the one at Scagh**. That would explain the negative result cleanly, and it retrospectively justifies the caution already recorded: *"'James Gleeson, farmer' on the 1864 certificate is weak evidence."* It was weak for exactly this reason — there was another one, twenty-five miles away, in the townland Patrick moved to.

⚠️ **Not established.** Two men named James Gleeson farming in one townland is not impossible, and the age gap (Bridget stated 22 in 1864 → b. ~1842; Timothy 31 in 1867 → b. ~1836) is plausible for siblings but proves nothing.

### And it sharpens the corridor question

Scagh in the 1850s–60s held **a cluster of Gleesons**: Timothy senior (the 1851 occupier), a **James Gleeson farming there** with at least one and probably two adult children, **Bridget**, and from 1859 **Patrick**. Patrick was not an isolated incomer to an empty townland — he arrived into a Gleeson settlement.

**Whether he was related to them is still the open question**, and it is now the *central* one. The consanguinity dispensation says he was related to *somebody* in the Croom–Croagh area. This is the most concentrated set of candidates yet found.

## 5. A note on the earlier block, now resolved

`marriages_1867/11492/8224682.pdf` **hangs pdf.js**, exactly as the 1864 and 1879 returns did.

**This is now confirmed across three independent samples (1864, 1867, 1879) and should be treated as settled: IrishGenealogy civil marriage returns defeat pdf.js as a class.** Do not spend turns re-testing it.

Chrome's own PDF viewer decodes the file — the sidebar thumbnail renders correctly — but the **main viewer pane is not captured by the browser extension's screenshot** (it returns an empty dark frame; the surrounding viewer chrome and the sidebar thumbnail *are* captured, so the page is a separate compositing layer). ⚠️ **Correct the note added earlier on 2026-08-20 claiming the extension "can now screenshot Chrome's PDF viewer" — it cannot.** That earlier apparent success was a scrolled-DOM artefact, not the plugin surface.

**Desktop screen capture at `read` tier is the reliable route**, and it worked here at 40% viewer zoom, reading a full register page with four entries legibly. Note that the grant **does not survive a device MCP disconnect/reconnect** and has to be re-requested if the connection blips mid-session.
