## 2. The headline finding

**Catherine Gleeson's father is named, in a civil document, as *Patrick Gleeson, Farmer*.**

This is the first record in the file that states the relationship directly. Until now the chain to Patrick ran:

- the 1862 baptism (*"Catharina filia Patritii Gleeson & Margaritae Moloney de Scagh"*) — names the parents, but is a parish record of her **birth**, not of her adult identity;
- the 1879 **Catholic** marriage entry — names **no fathers at all** (Latin registers don't);
- the American records — a death certificate, a headstone, and an obituary, all third-hand.

The gap was that nothing tied *the woman who married John Hayes* to *Patrick's daughter* except the parish register's silence and the absence of a rival. This closes that gap from the Irish side.

**Recommended status change:** Catherine as Patrick's daughter moves from strong inference to **[T1] — directly documented**.

**Residual caution, stated plainly:** the certificate names **no mother**, so it does not by itself link the bride to *Margaret Moloney*. What it establishes is that Catherine Gleeson of Croom, a farmer's daughter, was the daughter of a Patrick Gleeson who was a farmer. The identification with **our** Patrick rests on that plus: same forename, same surname, same parish, same occupation, and a daughter of the right name marrying at the right date — against a marriage register in which, per the 1864–1884 sweep, **no second Patrick Gleeson exists**. That is about as tight as Irish civil records get, but it is convergence, not a single document saying so.

## 3. The Farmer → Shopkeeper pivot just got much tighter

Patrick's documented occupational sequence:

| Date | Occupation | Residence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 Sep 1859 | Farmer | "Sean" (= Scagh) | Petty Sessions |
| 14 Jun 1864 | Farmer | Scaugh | Petty Sessions |
| 26 Apr 1869 | Farmer | Scough | Petty Sessions |
| 15 Dec 1872 | Farmer | Skagh | Mary's civil birth reg |
| 27 Sep 1875 | Farmer | Skagh | Margaret's civil birth reg |
| **29 Nov 1879** | **Farmer** | **Croom** | **this record** |
| 3 Nov 1881 | **Shopkeeper** | Croom | Bridget's civil birth reg |
| 7 Dec 1897 | Carrier | Croom | death certificate |

**The pivot window collapses from 15 Dec 1872 – 3 Nov 1881 (almost nine years) to 29 Nov 1879 – 3 Nov 1881 (under two).**

### But do NOT read the "Croom" residence as proof the family had left Skagh

Column 7 gives **Croom** for both parties. That is ambiguous and should not be over-read:

- **Against a move:** Patrick is still styled **Farmer** in this very entry. A Main Street shopkeeper's daughter would not normally be entered as *Farmer's daughter*. The registrar also records the *bride's* residence, and she was living at home.
- **For registrar's shorthand:** the same page enters residences at district level elsewhere — entry 92 gives "Limerick City" and "Fedamore". Marriage returns routinely give the town or district rather than the townland, whereas *birth* registrations (which gave "Skagh" in 1872 and 1875) name the townland because the birth happened there.

**Calibrated reading:** the family was **probably still at Skagh** in November 1879, with "Croom" being district-level shorthand. **Possible**, not established. The 1881 registration remains the first hard evidence of the village address.

## 4. John Hayes's family — new, and it reframes the emigration

Previously John Hayes was only "a local Croom-area man". Now:

- **John Hayes, Weaver**, of Croom
- **father Peter Hayes, Weaver**, of Croom

A **weaving** household, two generations deep — a **trade, not land**. That matters for the story. Irish handloom weaving was in terminal collapse through the second half of the nineteenth century, undercut by mechanised British cloth. A young weaver marrying in 1879 was entering a trade with no future.

This supplies, for the first time, an **economic motive** for the couple's emigration around 1895 that does not depend on guesswork, and it explains the otherwise odd sixteen-year gap: they married into a dying trade, stayed while it was still survivable, and left when it wasn't.

**New searchable lead:** **Peter Hayes, weaver, Croom** — testable against Griffith's Valuation, the 1901 census (if he lived), and civil death records. Also worth checking whether the Hayes weavers appear in the Croom baptism register as a distinct household from the other Hayes families already catalogued.

## 5. Smaller findings

**"Full age" is formulaic here, not a lie.** Catherine was **17 years and 10 months** on her wedding day. "Full age" means 21 or over, and a bride under 21 required recorded parental consent. Before reading anything into that: **entry 92 on the same page also gives "full age" for both parties**, and the phrase recurs down the page. This registrar wrote it as a default. The honest conclusion is not that the couple misstated her age but that **this certificate yields no usable age for anyone on it** — do not add it to the birth-year table.

**She was "Kate".** The certificate records her as *Catherine* in the body and **Kate** in the signature block, matching the civil index. Both forms are hers.

**The witnesses are confirmed, and the Shanahan wife is Catherine/Kate.** *Michael Shanahan* and *Kate Shanahan* — corroborating the Catholic register's *"Coram Michaele et Catherinâ Shanahan"*. Since Michael Shanahan married on 22 September 1879, **Kate Shanahan was his wife of ten weeks**. This independently supports reading the September bride's forename as **Catherine**, and therefore supports the correction already recorded: she is **not** "Catherine Lynch".

**A possible source of the old "Lynch" error.** Entry **92 on this same page** is *John O'Toole × **Jane Lynch***, Fedamore, 9 October 1879. A Lynch bride sitting directly above our entry is a plausible origin for the "Catherine Lynch" reading that entered `facts.md`. Speculative — recorded because it is cheap to note and would explain a otherwise puzzling error.

**⚠️ Michael Shanahan, witness 1879 → Michael Shanahan, Assistant Registrar 1897 and 1922?** Patrick's death (7 Dec 1897) and Margaret's death (18 May 1922) were **both registered by "Michl Shanahan, Asst Registrar"**. The witness at their daughter's wedding in 1879 bears the same name in the same small district. If they are one man, the family friend who stood up at Catherine's marriage went on to record both her parents' deaths — a twenty-five-year thread through the family's civil record. **Unproven.** Shanahan is common in Croom and no document yet links the two roles. Worth one check against the 1901 census for a Michael Shanahan at Croom with a registrar's occupation.

## 6. What this does not do

- **It does not touch James J. Gleeson.** His Irish life remains blank between his baptism (23 Dec 1863) and his Wisconsin marriage (23 Nov 1892).
- **It does not name Margaret Moloney**, so it does not independently confirm Catherine's mother.
- **It does not give a usable age** for Catherine (see §5), so the birth-year question is still settled solely by the 1862 baptism.

## 7. Method note — this PDF defeats pdf.js

`marriages_1879/11085/8056995.pdf` **hangs the pdf.js renderer indefinitely** at every configuration tried: v3.11 at 3200 px, v3.11 at 1600 px, and v4.0.379 at 2400 px all reach `rendering` and never complete. This is the same signature as `marriages_1864/11622/8278811.pdf`.

**Conclusion: this is a class of IrishGenealogy scan, not a one-off.** Treat pdf.js as unavailable for civil marriage returns and go straight to Chrome's own PDF viewer.

Two further notes for the pipeline:

- **Ferrying the raw PDF bytes out of the browser does not work.** Fetching the file in-page and base64-encoding it for transfer to a container (where poppler decodes CCITT without trouble) is blocked by the Chrome extension's anti-exfiltration filter on encoded payloads. Do not attempt to disguise the payload to evade it.
- **The extension *can* screenshot Chrome's PDF viewer** in this session, contrary to the earlier note that it returned a blank dark frame. `computer` scroll and zoom both worked directly on the PDF plugin surface. Desktop capture at `read` tier was granted and also worked. Try the extension first next time.
