| Ship | **Chicago** (Guion/National Line) |

### ⚠️ Two cautions on this record

**(a) I initially misread the results grid.** In the search-results table the last column is the **ship name**, not the destination — the sibling rows read *Arizona*, *Alaska*, *Abyssinia*, *Italy*, *City of Brussels*, all 1880s steamers. I reported "destination Chicago" off that column before opening the record. It happens to be right, but only by coincidence: the ship was *also* named **Chicago**. **The detail page carries a separate Destination field, and it independently reads "Chicago, Illinois."** Verified on the image (below) that the manifest really does record per-passenger destinations.

**(b) The identification is NOT proven.** What ties this to our man is: forename, surname, Irish, age 19 vs. our James baptised **21 Dec 1863** at Croom, and a Queenstown embarkation. That is **a bare forename + surname + nationality + approximate age** — precisely the identifier strength this project has been burned by three times in this session alone (James Moloney the shopkeeper; "Maria Molony 1865"; the 1918 deed). There is no townland, no parent, no companion, no witness linking it to Croom.
**It should be recorded as a strong candidate, not a fact.** The tree currently carries it as a fact.

## 3. What the manifest image itself shows — and its hard limit

I opened the image and read it directly (Ancestry viewer, zoomed, canvas brightness-boosted).

**Confirmed:** the sheet's columns are *line no. / name / age / sex / occupation / country of citizenship / **destination** / … *. Legible destinations in the readable upper block include *New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Philadelphia*. In the lower block the destination column reads, in order: **Chicago — Philadelphia — Boston — California — Fort Wayne — Minnesota.**

So "Chicago, Illinois" is a genuine per-passenger entry, not an artifact of the ship's name.

**❌ The blocking problem:** the **left-hand third of the lower block — the name, age and occupation columns for exactly those rows — is a dead black light-leak on the NARA film.** It is clipped to pure black in the delivered tiles; brightness of ×2, ×5 and ×9 recovers nothing, because there is no signal to recover. The names beside "Chicago" **cannot be read from this frame.**

What that costs us: **occupation**, and **whether he travelled with anyone** — the two things the image could have added.

**⚠️ And a correction to something I said earlier this session:** I called the manifest's *"nearest relative in Ireland"* column "the prize." **There is no such column on an 1883 customs list.** That field appears on the *Immigration Service* manifests standardised from 1893 (and in its full form from 1906). I was describing a 20th-century document. Withdrawn.

**Practical read: the manifest is now spent.** Nothing further is extractable from it.

## 5. What this changes about the naturalization thread

The naturalization file (Tier-1 #1) was wanted mainly to date the emigration and name the ship. **The tree already has both**, from 1883. That was already my reason for withdrawing my "single most valuable document in the project" claim; this reinforces it.

The naturalization would still be worth having for one thing the manifest cannot give: a **birth town**. But he was naturalized before 1900 → pre-1906 → a county-court record that typically records neither.

**Recommendation: demote Tier-1 #1.** Promote instead:
- the **Leader-Telegram obituary of 30 Jan 1946** — a second, independently-written notice, and the only remaining source likely to name an Irish place in his own family's words;
- the **Croagh baptism sponsors** for the two candidate Margarets, which is still the live discriminator on the open Margaret question.
