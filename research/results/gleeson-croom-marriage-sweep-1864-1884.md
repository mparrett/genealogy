# Croom Marriage Register — Gleeson Sweep, October 1864 to June 1884

**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Plan:** `research/analysis/catherine-hayes-vetting-plan-2026-08-18.md`, Q1.1
**Source:** NLI Catholic Parish Registers, Croom, microfilm **02427/07** (`vtls000634977`)
**Frames inspected at image level:** 154, 155, 156, 157, 158, 159, 160, 161 — every frame covering Oct 1864 through the end of the volume's marriage run on 24 June 1884.
**Method:** each frame rendered at native resolution (1500 px page width) and read in two overlapping horizontal bands at full magnification, then re-cropped at 2–4× on every Gleeson occurrence to fix the reading. No transcription or index was allowed to stand in for the image.

Two consequences. First, the "Johannam Gleeson appearing as a witness, frame 137, February 1874" in the old review is **February 1854**, not 1874, and belongs to a different generation. Second, the marriage frames *are* broadly chronological from 154 onward, so the whole 1864–1884 period is covered by **eight frames**, not by a hand-sweep of the entire volume. The plan budgeted for the latter.

Small gaps exist between frames where the register itself skips: Jun 1871 → Nov 1871, Sep 1874 → Nov 1874, Jun 1879 → Sep 1879. These are gaps in the record, not in the search.

### Uncertain readings, left un-normalised

- **3 Mar 1867, bride's surname.** Reads *Noran* or *Horan*; the initial is formed like the H of "Hanrahan" on the following line but lacks its full loop. Recorded as written, not resolved.
- **3 Oct 1880, bride's surname.** The plan describes this entry as *Catherinam Feorey*. On the image the surname and the sponsors' shared surname both read **Toomey**, and the same hand writes an unambiguous "Thomam Toomey" on frame 159 (15 Jan 1875) and "Thomâ Toomey" (15 Jan 1876) for comparison. **Toomey is the better reading**; Feorey is retained as an alternative rather than discarded.
- **19 Jun 1869, first sponsor.** *Jacobo Mulcahy* is clear; the day of the month is written over a correction and reads 19.

## What this does and does not settle

### The reciprocal-witness pattern, in full

Both halves of it sit on the same frame, two months apart:

- **22 September 1879** — Michael Shanahan marries Catherine Synan; **Patrick Gleeson** is a witness.
- **29 November 1879** — John Hayes marries **Catherine Gleeson**; **Michael and Catherine Shanahan** are the witnesses.

The plan treated this as the claim under test. The sweep now adds that the Gleeson–Shanahan association is **not confined to 1879**. It is visible a decade earlier:

- **19 June 1869** — Anna Gleeson witnesses the marriage of **Jacobus Shanahan**.
- **4 August 1869** — **Patrick Shanahan** witnesses the marriage of Margaret Gleeson.

So the 1879 exchange is the late instance of a standing relationship between two families who had been standing up at each other's weddings since at least 1869. That is a materially stronger contextual argument than a single reciprocal pair, and it does not depend on the uniqueness of the forename Patrick.

### Applying the plan's Q1 decision rule

The rule reads: *"No second plausible Patrick in the Croom register: the reciprocal-witness argument remains useful, but should still be phrased as strong contextual evidence rather than sole identification proof."*

That is the branch we are on. **Recommended wording: the 1879 witness pattern is strong contextual evidence consistent with the identification, and is not by itself proof.** The word "confirms" should not be used, and neither should any downgrade to mere consistency — no competing Patrick was found to justify one.

Note what the register does *not* supply: the 22 September 1879 entry gives no residence, no occupation and no relationship for the witness. It says "Patritio Gleeson" and nothing more. The identification rests on the absence of a rival bearing that name in the same register, plus the family association evidenced around it — not on anything internal to the entry.

### A new lead: Margaret Gleeson and Thomas Moloney, 18 October 1875

At the marriage of Stephen Leo and Mary Donovan the two witnesses are **Thomas Moloney and Margaret Gleeson**. Catherine's mother was **Margaret Gleeson formerly Moloney**, and the couple's daughter Margaret had been born three weeks earlier, on 27 September 1875, and registered on 2 November 1875.

A married woman standing as witness alongside a man of her own birth surname is exactly the pattern one expects if this is Margaret (Moloney) Gleeson appearing with a Moloney relative. It is **not established** — the entry gives no residence and no relationship, and another Margaret Gleeson had married Patrick O'Dea in 1869 and would have been going by O'Dea. But it is the first plausible sighting of Catherine's mother in the marriage register in her own right, and **Thomas Moloney** is a name worth carrying into the Moloney work.

### Coverage limits — stated plainly

- **Frames 126–153 (Sep 1844 – Oct 1864) were not swept in this pass.** A Gleeson who married in, say, 1855 could still be an adult in 1879, so this window is not irrelevant to the question of who else bore the name locally. Thirteen of those frames are already on disk in `assets/external/nli-croom/`, and the 2026-04 review covered roughly 37 images of that range, finding the August 1864 Bridget Gleeson marriage and a February 1854 Johanna Gleeson witness. A clean re-sweep of 126–153 to the standard used here is outstanding.
- **This volume's marriage entries stop on 24 June 1884.** The plan already noted that the 1884–1885 extension needs a later volume; the free NLI set for Croom lists microfilms 02427/04, /05 and /06 alongside /07, and none has been checked for a post-June-1884 continuation.
- **The register was searched by eye for the surname in every form encountered, not by index.** Latinised forms found in use here are Gleeson only; no Glesson, Gleason or abbreviated form appeared in frames 154–161.
