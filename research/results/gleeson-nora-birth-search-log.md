- The family's residence at that date: **Skagh**, Croom registration district, County Limerick.
- Patrick's occupation at that date: **farmer**.
- Patrick was literate enough to act as informant and sign as "Patt Gleeson".

## Consequences for the plan

### Q3.3 — the twin hypothesis is now contradicted, not merely cautioned

The amended plan flagged a chronology caution: Margaret's *baptism* of 29 September 1875 sits awkwardly with a twin allegedly born in January or February 1876. The civil registration removes the ambiguity by supplying the **birth** date rather than the baptism date. Margaret was **born 27 September 1875**. A twin sister born in January or February 1876 is impossible; the two dates are three to five months apart.

Adjacent baptism entries around 29 September 1875 should still be inspected for a second child of the same parents, per the plan, but the entry should no longer be described as a possible twin of Nora. Recommended wording: **contradicted**.

### Q3.1/Q3.2 — Nora is not resolved, and one negative is now on record

No Nora, Norah, Honora, Hanora, Honor, Hannah or Anne Gleeson appears in the Croom civil birth index for **1876** under the spelling "Gleeson". The only 1876 female Gleeson birth indexed in Croom is Teresa, now eliminated.

**This is a partial negative only.** It does not license the conclusion that no birth was registered, because this pass did **not** search:

- surname variants and mistranscriptions (Gleason, Glesson, Gleison, Glisson, Gleerson, and OCR-damaged forms);
- adjacent registration districts along the Croom boundary;
- late registrations filed under a later year;
- the "unknown"/unnamed female entries, which are indexed without a forename.

Per the plan's evidence rules, absence under one spelling in one district is not absence of a registration.

### Q1 — an incidental finding worth carrying forward

The Teresa record documents a **second Gleeson farming household inside Croom registration district**: Michael Gleeson and Mary McNamara of **Fanningstown**, distinct from Patrick and Margaret Moloney of **Skagh**. This does not itself produce a competing *Patrick*, and under the plan's Q1 decision rule a broad-area same-surname occurrence is not grounds for downgrading anything. But it does establish that Gleeson households in the Croom civil district were separable by townland, and **Skagh** is now a firm discriminator to apply against every Patrick Gleeson candidate found in the marriage-register sweep and in Griffith's Valuation.

## Method note

The container running this session has no network route to irishgenealogy.ie, registers.nli.ie or iserver.nli.ie, so the curl pipeline in `research/analysis/nli-parish-register-pipeline.md` could not be used. Images were instead decoded in the browser: the register PDF was fetched same-origin, rendered with pdf.js at 3200–4864 px page width, and read in magnified crops. Rendering above ~3200 px width is unreliable — it stalls the renderer — so **3200 px is the working setting**. This route is reproducible for any IrishGenealogy PDF and for the NLI JPEG endpoints.
