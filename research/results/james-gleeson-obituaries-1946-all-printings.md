> James J. Gleeson, 82, living at the home of his daughter, Mrs. Ray Theisen, Waite Park, died at 3:30 a.m. today.

## ⭐⭐ What is genuinely new

### 1. "Croom, County Limerick" — stated outright

Earlier this session I characterised the obituary as *"only mentions 'from Croom'"*. **That was wrong, and it undersold our best US-side anchor.** Printing 3 gives **"Croom, County Limerick, Ireland"** in full, together with a birth **day and month — 21 December — that matches the Croom baptism register exactly** (baptised 23 Dec 1863, born 21 Dec 1863). Only the year is off by one.

A US newspaper naming the exact Irish parish *and* county *and* the correct calendar day is strong corroboration that the Croom baptism is our man. Correction logged.

### 2. ⭐ "Porters Mills" — his first American workplace, and a new research target

**Porter's Mills** was a sawmill company town on the Chippewa River just below Eau Claire, Wisconsin — a genuine place, not a garbling. This is the first specific American employment we have for him, and it is independently corroborated by printing 2 ("worked around Eau Claire for a time after coming to this country").

**It also corroborates the 1900 census, which gives his occupation as "Head Sawier" — i.e. head sawyer.** A man who learned the trade at Porter's Mills.

**Why this matters for the open emigration-date question:** it puts him in the Eau Claire district as a young unmarried man *before* the 1892 marriage and the Mondovi farm. That is a searchable place and period — **Eau Claire city directories** and the **1885 Wisconsin state census** — and either could date his arrival independently. ⚠️ Note the tension: the tree places a *Minneapolis* residence on **1 May 1885** (a Minnesota state census). Both can be true of a mobile young labourer, but they are not the same place.

### 3. ⚠️ Burial: **Riverside** cemetery, not Sacred Heart

The two papers disagree:

| Printing | Burial |
|---|---|
| *St. Cloud Times* | "funeral service and **burial in Sacred Heart cemetery**" |
| *Leader-Telegram* p.9 | "funeral services … at the **Sacred Heart church** … **Interment was in Riverside cemetery**" |

**Prefer the Leader-Telegram.** It is the local paper, it was written *after* the funeral rather than before, and it distinguishes the church from the cemetery — which is exactly the distinction the distant St. Cloud paper would collapse. The repo and tree both currently imply Sacred Heart.

**Consequence: if we want his grave, look at Riverside Cemetery, Mondovi first.** Note that Maria Ann Fitzgerald and her father James Fitzgerald are recorded in this repo as buried at *Sacred Heart* Cemetery, Mondovi — so the family used both, or one of those attributions is also wrong. Worth an hour on Find A Grave.

### 4. Confirmed: **twelve** children

Both substantial printings say twelve, five living. Our documented list runs to nine or ten. **Two or three children are unaccounted for** — presumably infant deaths at Mondovi, findable in Wisconsin birth/death records. Also confirmed: "A son, Joseph, died at the age of 17" = Joseph Patrick Gleeson, 1900–1917. ✓

## ⚠️ Errors in the printings — do not cite these figures

| Printed | Actual | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "born **Dec. 21, 1864**" | born 21 Dec **1863** | Day and month exact; year off by one. Matches the tree's "21 Dec 1864" alternates — they all trace to this obituary. |
| "In **Nov. 1876**, he married Maria Fitzgerald" | **23 Nov 1892** | **Impossible** — Maria was born 1865 and would have been 11. The *month* is right. A typesetting error, almost certainly for 1892. |
| "died Aug. 2, **1837**" (Bina O'Brien) | 2 Aug **1937** | Obvious misprint; Matt flagged it in his Bear note. |
| "In **Jan. 1928**, married Mrs. Bina O'Brien" | tree: **14 Jan 1929** | One or the other is wrong; unresolved. |
| "**83**" (p.2) vs "**82**" (St. Cloud) | 82 | Born Dec 1863, died Jan 1946. |
| "**nine** grandchildren" (St. Cloud) vs "**ten**" (p.9) | — | Five days apart, two different counts. |
| "Mrs. Gleeson has been dead **28** years" | Maria d. 1916 (30 yrs); Sabina d. 1937 (9 yrs) | Matches neither wife. |
| "Mrs. John **Hayes**… survives" | Catherine died **1 Nov 1944** | Already analysed — stale family knowledge, not a misidentification. |

**Standing observation:** across three printings of one man's death, in five days, by two newspapers, we get **two ages, two grandchild counts, two cemeteries, a birth year off by one, and a marriage year off by sixteen.** This is a useful calibration on how much weight a single newspaper figure can carry anywhere else in this project.
