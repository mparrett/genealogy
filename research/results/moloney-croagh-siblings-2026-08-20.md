# Margaret Moloney's natal family — Croagh siblings recovered

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Method:** Ancestry collection 61039 (indexed from the NLI Croagh/Kilfinny register), searched on **both parents** — `father=James_Molony` + `mother=Catherine_Kenny`, county facet Limerick.
**Why now:** the Scagh Gleesons turned out to be neighbours rather than kin (`gleeson-timothy-skagh-2026-08-20.md` §8), which removed the Gleeson-side lead on the 1860 consanguinity. Margaret's side had never been worked with the index.

| Child | Baptism | Indexed surname spelling |

| **Margaret** ("Margt") — *our ancestor* | **31 Dec 1836** | Molony |

**Margaret's natal family goes from a lone baptism to a documented sibling set of four.** Note the surname is spelled three different ways across four entries by the same parish — a useful caution, and consistent with the "spelling drifts freely in Irish parish records" note already in `facts.md`.

⚠️ **The register begins in 1836**, the year Margaret was baptised, so **any older siblings are invisible** — she may not be the eldest. Nothing appears after January 1843; whether that is the end of the family or an indexing gap is untested.

## ⭐ Finding 1 — the naming pattern names Margaret's grandfather

Under the Irish convention the **first son takes the paternal grandfather's name**. The first son here is **Michael** (1840).

**So James Molony's father was probably MICHAEL Molony** — i.e. **Michael Molony is likely Margaret's paternal grandfather**, born c. 1775–1790. [T2, naming-pattern inference]

The second son is **James** (1843), which under the same convention points to the **maternal** grandfather also being James, or simply repeats the father's name.

### ⚠️ This reshapes the Jeremiah/Kate reconciliation

The 2026-06-09 reconciliation established that **Jeremiah Molony of Ballymackeamore** (the 1918 deed party, and Kate's father) was baptised 28 Oct 1842 to **MICHAEL Molony + Honora Minihan** — *not* to James + Catherine Kenny. It then hypothesised: *"if Michael Molony (Jeremiah's father, and Margaret's 1860 marriage witness) and James Molony (Margaret's father) were brothers, then Margaret and Jeremiah were first cousins."*

That hypothesis is **still live but should be re-framed**, because there are now **two** distinct Michael Molonys in play:

1. **Michael Molony**, Jeremiah's father (m. Honora Minihan) — the man the old hypothesis proposed as James's brother.
2. **Michael Molony**, **Margaret's own brother**, baptised 13 Dec 1840 — newly documented here.
3. And, inferentially, **Michael Molony the grandfather** — James's own father, per the naming rule.

A recurring Michael in this family makes "the Michaels must be the same man" reasoning much weaker than it looked. The **grandfather** reading offers a cleaner explanation of the name's recurrence than the brother reading does.

---

## ⭐ Finding 2 — the 1860 marriage witness was probably Margaret's brother

Patrick and Margaret's marriage (Croagh, **18 Nov 1860**) is witnessed by **"Michaele Moloney"**. `facts.md` currently reads this as corroboration of the Moloney kin network generally, and the June work tentatively associated it with Jeremiah's father.

**Margaret's brother Michael was baptised 13 December 1840** — making him **19 years and 11 months old** at the wedding. A brother just short of twenty standing witness at his sister's marriage is the single most ordinary thing in an Irish parish register.

**Recommended reading: the 1860 witness is Margaret's brother Michael [T2],** in preference to the older Michael. This is a simpler explanation and it uses a documented person rather than an inferred relationship.

## 🔧 Method note

Croagh's register is in **English**, unlike Croom's Latin — so search `father=James_Molony`, *not* `Jacobi`. Getting this backwards costs a round of empty results in either direction; **check which language a parish's register uses before searching the index.**

Searching on **both parents at once** is what worked. Single-parent searches drowned in relevance ranking — `mother=Catherine_Kenny` alone returned no Croagh rows in the top fifty. The index normalises the *parents'* forenames while leaving the *child's* surname spelled as written, which is why one query returned Molony, Moloney and "Mollone y" together.

⚠️ `f-F0003042` is **not** a parish facet in this collection — passing it returns near-nothing. Only `f-F0003040` (county) works.

## ⚠️⚠️ CORRECTION, same day — Finding 3 is WRONG. The Croom shopkeeper is not Margaret's brother.

**The 1874 marriage certificate has now been read**, and it contradicts the upgrade made above. Recording the reversal in full rather than editing Finding 3 away, because the reasoning that produced it was a textbook trap.

### The certificate

Limerick SR district, marriage **No. 44**, GRID **2844557**, image `marriage_returns/marriages_1874/11248/8125424.pdf` p.24. Read at image level 2026-08-20.

> **1874. Marriage solemnized at the Roman Catholic Cathedral of Limerick, Registrar's District of City No. 3, Union of Limerick, County of the City of Limerick.**

| Col. | Field | Party 1 | Party 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | When married | **June 8th 1874** | |
| 3 | Name | **James Moloney** | **Honoria Quaid** |
| 4 | **Age** | **27** | **20** |
| 5 | Condition | Bachelor | Spinster |
| 6 | Rank | **Farmer** | — |
| 7 | **Residence** | **BALLYMACKAMORE** | same as above |
| 8 | **Father** | **MICHAEL MOLONEY** | David Quaid |
| 9 | Father's rank | Farmer | do. |

Celebrant Geo. Quaid. Witnesses **James Purcell** and **William Quaid**.

### Why this kills Finding 3

- **Age 27 → born c. 1847.** Margaret's brother James was baptised **8 January 1843** and would have been **31**.
- **His father is MICHAEL Moloney, not James.**

So the man who married Honoria Quaid is **not** Margaret's brother. Assuming the 1874 couple is the 1881 Croom couple — the same rare name pair, in an adjacent parish, seven years later — **the 1881 Croom shopkeeper is a son of Michael Moloney of Ballymackeamore.**

⚠️ That last step is **not proven**. Two distinct James Moloney × Honoria Quaid couples in adjoining parishes would be a remarkable coincidence, but it has not been excluded. **The certificate proves the 1874 couple's parentage; the link to the Croom shopkeeper remains [T2].**

### 🔁 The methodological failure, stated plainly

Finding 3 above reasoned: *facts.md predicted a brother named James; a brother named James exists; therefore the James in the Croom record is him.* The age fitted, the trade fitted, the naming rule fitted.

**It was still wrong, because a third James Moloney existed.** This is now the **third** time in one day that a conclusion in this project has been overturned by a second (or third) man of the same name — after James Gleeson of Hospital vs James Gleeson of Croom, and Timothy senior vs Timothy junior.

**The standing rule holds and should be applied harder:** a bare forename + surname + occupation is not evidence. Do not upgrade an identification on a naming-convention prediction plus a plausible age. **Get the father's name.**

## ⭐ But the certificate gives something better than it took away

**Ballymackeamore** is not a new name in this project. It is the townland of:

- **Jeremiah Molony**, the counterparty in the **1918 Registry of Deeds memorial** — Indenture of Release, *Jeremiah Molony of Ballymacamore, farmer* → **Margaret Gleeson of Main Street, Croom, widow**. Jeremiah was baptised **28 Oct 1842 to MICHAEL Molony and Honora Minihan**.
- **Kate Moloney**, Jeremiah's daughter — mother of **Ellen "Helen" Kelly**, born 3 Mar 1905 at Ballymackeamore, who as **Helen Ryan** was informant and executor on **Catherine Hayes's 1944 death certificate in New York**.

**James Moloney of Ballymackeamore, born c.1847 to Michael Moloney, is therefore very probably Jeremiah's younger brother** — same father's forename, same townland, five years apart. [T2]

### Which means a real, multi-generational tie comes into focus — just not the one we expected

| Date | Event | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **17 Feb 1881** | **Patrick Gleeson stands godfather** to John, son of James Moloney and Honoria Quaid, at Croom | Godfather to **Jeremiah's brother's son** |
| **10 Jan 1918** | **Jeremiah Molony of Ballymackeamore** releases a mortgage to **Margaret Gleeson**, widow | The same family, 37 years later |
| **1 Nov 1944** | **Helen Ryan**, Jeremiah's granddaughter, informs and executes **Catherine Hayes's death** on Staten Island | The same family, another 26 years and an ocean later |

**The Gleesons had a sustained relationship with the Ballymackeamore Molonys running from at least 1881 to 1944, across three generations and two continents.** That is a genuinely important structural fact — and it is **not** via Margaret's natal family (James Molony × Catherine Kenny of Croagh), which is a different Molony line entirely.

⚠️ **This does not restore the retracted 2026-06-08 claims.** Kate is still *not* Margaret's niece and Jeremiah is still *not* Margaret's brother — the 2026-06-09 reconciliation stands. What changes is that the *relationship* is now visible much earlier than 1918 and is denser than a single deed suggested.

**The obvious question this raises:** why was Patrick godfathering into that family in 1881, and why did they hold the mortgage on Margaret's shop in 1918? A sustained cross-generational bond of that kind usually has kinship behind it — and **Patrick and Margaret's 1860 consanguinity dispensation says they shared an ancestral couple c.1710–1750 whose surname is unknown**. The Ballymackeamore Molonys are now a candidate line for that, and they were not on the list before.
