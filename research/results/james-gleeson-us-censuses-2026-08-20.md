## ⭐ Finding 1 — December 1863 matches the baptism exactly

The 1900 census is the only US record that gives his **birth month**. It says **December 1863**. His Croom baptism is **23 December 1863**.

**This is the strongest US-side confirmation of the identification in the whole file** — better than the 1946 obituary, which gives a birthplace but no date, and better than the 1892 marriage record, which gives parents but no birth date. An Irish-born James Gleeson in Buffalo County reporting a December 1863 birth, with parents Patrick Gleeson and Margaret Moloney on his marriage record, is our man beyond reasonable argument.

---

## ⭐⭐ Finding 2 — he was naturalized BEFORE 1900, so the file is pre-1906

**This answers the question the search was run to answer, and the answer is disappointing.**

US naturalization was **standardised in September 1906**. From then on, petitions are rich documents — exact birth date, **birth town**, ship, port, arrival date, physical description. Before 1906 there was no federal form: records were kept by whichever county or state court did the swearing, and are typically a **single line** giving name, country of former allegiance, and date.

The 1900 census records him as **already naturalized**. So:

> **⚠️ The naturalization file will almost certainly be a sparse pre-1906 county record, not the detailed federal petition.**

**I over-sold this.** It has been described in this project's notes as *"the single most valuable document in the project"* and *"the one thing that would put a date and a ship on the emigration."* **That framing was wrong and is withdrawn.** It rested on assuming a post-1906 filing without checking. A one-line county entry will give a date and a court and nothing about Croom.

It is still worth pulling — a date and court are real, and a **Declaration of Intention** (often filed years earlier, sometimes separately preserved) occasionally names the vessel. But it drops from Tier 1 to a modest Tier 2 item.

## ⚠️ Finding 3 — the two censuses disagree on the emigration year

| Source | Immigration year |
|---|---|
| **1900 census** | **1885** (with "years in US = 15", internally consistent) |
| 1920 census | 1882 |
| 1946 obituary | *"came to the US as a young man"* — no year |

**Prefer 1885.** The 1900 return is fifteen years closer to the event and carries a second field that corroborates it. The 1920 figure is thirty-eight years after the fact.

**Working window for a passenger search: 1882–1885**, with 1885 the leading year. He would have been 18–21.

*(For scale: the file previously assumed "~1883" from the obituary's "young man". That was a guess; it now has documentary bounds.)*

## Finding 4 — another occupation shift, and a household timeline

**Head Sawyer in 1900 → Farmer and employer by 1920.** A sawmill worker with a mortgaged farm became a debt-free farm owner in twenty years.

That is the **third** Farmer↔trade shift documented in this family today, after Patrick (Farmer → Shopkeeper → Carrier) and James Moloney (Farmer → Shopkeeper). **Occupation continues to be a poor discriminator in this family and should not be used to accept or reject an identification.**

Also established: **Maria/Mary Fitzgerald died between 1900 and 1920** — he is married in 1900 and widowed in 1920. And **Joseph P Gleeson**, born about January 1900, appears in the 1900 household but is **not** among the five surviving children in the 1946 obituary.
