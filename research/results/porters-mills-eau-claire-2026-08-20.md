# Porter's Mills and Eau Claire — the first American years

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Origin:** the *Leader-Telegram* of 2 Feb 1946 says James *"came to America when a young man and **was employed for a time at what was then known as Porters Mills and Eau Claire**"*, independently echoed by the 30 Jan notice: *"He worked around Eau Claire for a time after coming to this country."*

---

## 1. What Porter's Mills actually was

A **sawmill company town in Eau Claire County, about four miles downriver from Eau Claire on the Chippewa River**, also called Porterville.

| | |
|---|---|
| First sawmill | **1863**, built by Charles Warner |
| Owners | Porter, Brown & Meredith (1864–66) → **Gilbert E. Porter** rebuilt after an 1866 fire → **Delos R. Moon** partner from 1867 → the **NORTHWESTERN LUMBER COMPANY** |
| Peak | **c. 1890** — nearly **500 workers**, town population **over 1,100**, predominantly **Scandinavian** immigrants |
| 1888 output | 45,000,000 ft of lumber · 25,000,000 shingles · 10,000,000 lath |
| Company town | **32 houses for married workers**, boarding for single men, schools, churches, stores, and a **dry-town policy** |
| Decline | Timber upstream ran out; scaling back from **1891**; **last mill dismantled 1899**; the village had **vanished by 1904** |

### ⭐ Two things this settles immediately

**(a) The obituary's phrase is two places, not one.** "Porters Mills **and** Eau Claire" names a **separate village** and **the city** — a normal two-stint career for a mill hand, not a single garbled placename.

**(b) ⚠️ Porter's Mills would NOT appear in an Eau Claire city directory.** It was its own village four miles downriver. **So a Porter's Mills stint is structurally invisible in the source I went on to search.** Any directory hit belongs to the *Eau Claire* half of the obituary's phrase, never the Porter's Mills half. Noting this **before** drawing conclusions, per the standing rule about knowing a source's coverage first.

**(c) The dates bracket cleanly.** The mill town was in full operation **1863–1899**, peaking around 1890. James arrived somewhere in **1882–1885** and married in **Nov 1892**, after which he farmed near Mondovi. A Porter's Mills stint therefore fits **c. 1883–1892**, squarely inside the boom. Nothing contradicts the obituary.

**⭐ New archival target:** the **Northwestern Lumber Company** (Porter's Mills) and **Valley Lumber Company** (Eau Claire) were substantial firms. Chippewa Valley lumber company records — payrolls and time books among them — are held at the **UW–Eau Claire McIntyre Library, Special Collections & Area Research Center**, and at the Wisconsin Historical Society. **A payroll naming him with a start date would date the emigration directly** — better than any census.

---

## 2. ⭐ The 1884 Eau Claire city directory — a strong candidate

**Eau Claire, Wisconsin, City Directory, 1884** (R. L. Polk & Co.), page **187**. Verbatim:

> **Gleason James, edger Valley Lumber Co, bds company's boarding house.**

*(Ancestry coll. 2469, record 806577771, image 13382157.)*

An **edger** runs the edger saw that trims boards square — a **sawmill trade**. *"bds company's boarding house"* means a **single man in company lodging**.

**Why this fits our James:**

| Fits | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Sawmill trade | The **1900 census gives his occupation as "Head Sawier"** — head sawyer. Edger → sawyer is the natural progression in a mill. |
| Eau Claire | Named in **both** obituary printings. |
| Single man in company lodging | He did not marry until **Nov 1892**. |
| Date | **1884** sits inside the documented 1882–1885 arrival window. |

### The 1885 entry, same man or not

**Eau Claire City Directory, 1885**, page **145**:

> **Gleason James, laborer, bds 1202 First ave.**

*(Record 813182150, image 13452160. ⓘ **Already saved to a person in Matt's tree** — so this one was found before; the 1884 edger entry appears not to have been.)*

Out of the company boarding house, into a rooming address, and demoted from a named trade to plain "laborer." Could be the same man between mill seasons, or a different one.

---

## 3. ⚠️⚠️ The competing candidate — and why I am NOT claiming this is him

**There is an established Gleason family in Eau Claire.** Across the directories 1884–1901 the collection shows:

**Enoch Gleason** (res 405 S Farwell) · **Elizabeth Gleason** (wife of Enoch) · **Charles C Gleason**, surveyor · **Charles R Gleason**, clerk, 405 S Farwell · **Frank C Gleason** · **Ella F Gleason** · **Lizzie Gleason** · **Thomas Gleason**, plumber · **Mrs Frank Gleason**, boarding-house keeper.

**A "James Gleason" in Eau Claire could easily belong to that family.** This is exactly the trap that has overturned three conclusions in this project. **Recorded as a candidate. Not as our man.**

Two further cautions:
- The spelling is **Gleason**, not Gleeson. *(Weak on its own — his own 1900 census entry is "Gleason" — but noted.)*
- The employer is **Valley Lumber Co**, an Eau Claire mill — **not** Northwestern Lumber Co at Porter's Mills.

### One pattern that genuinely argues *for* it

**James appears in 1884 and 1885 and then vanishes from the Eau Claire directories entirely**, while Enoch, Elizabeth, Charles R, Charles C, Frank C, Ella F and Lizzie all recur through 1889, 1893, 1895, 1901.

**A member of a settled local family recurs. A transient does not.** A young immigrant mill hand who worked two seasons, moved on — to Porter's Mills, or to Mondovi and a farm and a wife in 1892 — would look exactly like this. That is a **structural** argument rather than a nominal one, which makes it worth more than a name match. It is still not proof.

### ⚠️ And the inference I am deliberately NOT making

If the 1884 edger is ours, he was in Wisconsin by 1884 — which would **exclude the 1900 census's "1885"** and favour 1882 or 1883.

**That is circular and must not be used.** The identification is unproven; using it to narrow the arrival window, and then using the narrowed window to support the identification, is precisely the failure mode already caught once in this project (the withdrawn "9–10 year age-shaving pattern"). **The emigration year remains open at 1882–1885.**

---

## 4. The tests that would settle it

1. ⭐⭐ **Enoch Gleason's household in the 1880 and 1900 US censuses.** If Enoch has a son James of about the right age, that is the competing candidate and ours weakens sharply. If he does not, the transient reading strengthens a great deal. **Cheapest decisive test — do this first.**
2. ⭐ **Northwestern Lumber Co. and Valley Lumber Co. records** — UW–Eau Claire McIntyre Library, Special Collections & Area Research Center; Wisconsin Historical Society. A payroll or time book with his name and a start date would date the arrival outright.
3. **Buffalo County / Mondovi directories, 1886–1892** — where did he go after Eau Claire, and when did he reach Mondovi?
4. ❌ **1885 Wisconsin state census — do not bother.** The 1885 and 1895 Wisconsin state censuses record only the **head of household by name, plus tallies**. A single man boarding at a mill is invisible in them by construction. *(Checked the form's coverage before spending time on it.)*

## 5. ⚠️ The Enoch test — RUN 2026-08-20. It cleared the hypothesis I set, and surfaced a better rival.

### Part 1: the settled Gleason family does NOT contain a James — hypothesis not supported

**1880 US census, Eau Claire, Eau Claire Co., Wisconsin** (roll 1424, p.349b, ED 126):

| Household | Members |
|---|---|
| **Enoch Gleason**, 78, b. abt **1802 New York**, occupation **leather dresser**, Farwell St, dwelling 49 · parents both born **Massachusetts** | **Elizabeth D. Gleason**, 71, b. abt 1809 Massachusetts (wife). **No children at home.** |
| **Charles R. Gleason**, 49, b. abt **1831 New York** | **Francis Gleason**, 50, b. New York (wife) · **Frank Gleason**, 18, b. abt **1862 Wisconsin**, single, **laborer** (son) |

This is an **old Yankee family** — New York and Massachusetts, not Irish. Enoch and Elizabeth were **78 and 71** in 1880; Elizabeth would have been **54 in 1863**, so a son James born then is effectively impossible. Their son is Charles R.; **his** son is Frank, b. 1862.

**A search of the whole 1880 census for Gleasons in Eau Claire County born c.1853–1873 returns exactly one person: Frank Gleason (b. 1862), Charles R.'s son.**

✅ **So the hypothesis I set out to test — that the 1884 edger belongs to the settled Enoch Gleason family — is NOT supported.** That family has no James of the right age, in 1880 or anywhere in the directories.

### Part 2: ⚠️⚠️ …but a DIFFERENT competitor turned up, and he is better documented than ours

The same sweep returned, in the same city and census:

> **James Gleason · age 30 · b. abt 1850, CANADA · father's birthplace FRANCE · mother's birthplace FRANCE · single · occupation LUMBERMAN · Menomonee Street, Eau Claire · dwelling 21 · household of one**
> *(1880 US census, Ancestry coll. 6742, record 28380066)*

**A single French-Canadian lumberman living alone in Eau Claire in 1880.**

He fits *"Gleason James, edger Valley Lumber Co, bds company's boarding house"* **at least as well as our man, and arguably better**:

| | The Canadian lumberman | Our James |
|---|---|---|
| In Eau Claire before 1884? | **Yes — documented, 1880 census** | Unknown. Possibly not yet in the country. |
| In the lumber trade? | **Yes — "lumberman," 1880** | Inferred from an **undated** obituary phrase |
| Single, would board? | Yes — living alone, 1880 | Yes — married Nov 1892 |
| Age in 1884 | 34 | ~20 |

**Verdict: the 1884 edger identification is WEAKENED, not strengthened.** It should be downgraded from "strong candidate" to "one of at least two candidates, and not the better-documented one."

### Part 3: and the argument I made in §3 has to be withdrawn

I wrote that *"James appears in 1884 and 1885 and then vanishes, while the rest of the family recurs — settled families recur, transients do not."*

**That still holds as an observation, but it no longer points where I aimed it.** The vanishing James is just as consistent with a **French-Canadian lumberman moving on to the next camp** as with an Irish immigrant leaving for a Mondovi farm. The transient pattern discriminates *settled local family* from *transient*; it does **not** discriminate *our transient* from *someone else's*.

### ⚠️ And the structural point from §1 cuts against the whole line

**Porter's Mills was a separate village four miles downriver and is not covered by the Eau Claire city directory.** If our James's first years were spent at Porter's Mills, **he would not appear in these directories at all** — and neither 1884 nor 1885 would be him.

**Standing position: no directory entry is currently attributable to James J. Gleeson. The Eau Claire years remain documented only by the obituary.**

### What would still settle it

1. ⭐⭐ **Northwestern Lumber Co. / Valley Lumber Co. payrolls and time books** — UW–Eau Claire McIntyre Library, Special Collections & Area Research Center; Wisconsin Historical Society. **Now the only route likely to name him.** A payroll gives a name *and* a date, which is exactly what neither the directory nor the census can supply here.
2. Track the **Canada-born James Gleason** forward (1885/1895 WI state census, 1900 census). If he left Eau Claire before 1884, the directory entry reopens for ours. *(Attempted 2026-08-20 in the 1900 census; the birthplace facet would not bind and no Wisconsin match surfaced in the first page. Unresolved.)*
3. **Buffalo County / Mondovi directories 1886–1892** — approach the question from the Mondovi end instead.

**Method note:** this is what a test looks like when it works. It was cheap, it was decisive about the thing it was aimed at, and it changed the answer — just not in the direction I was hoping for. Recording the disappointment as carefully as the discovery is the whole point.
