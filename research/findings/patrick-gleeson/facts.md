## Evidence tier legend

Claims throughout this doc are tagged where confidence matters:

- **[T1]** — Directly documented: the cited record itself names the relationship (baptism naming parents, marriage naming father, census household relationship, obituary naming sibling/child, deed stating relationship).
- **[T2]** — Strong but inferential: identity established through convergent evidence (multiple records, naming patterns, kin-network behavior) but no single record directly states the claim.
- **[T3]** — Active lead: single-source or weak association; in research pipeline, not in the proved tree.

The earlier "⭐ LOCKED" annotations have been retired in favor of these tiers per the 2026-06-03 review (see [[gleeson-patrick-natal-family]] memory for the methodology shift).

    ### ⚠️⚠️ But do NOT present this as confirming a family tradition — the "tradition" is one hedged email

    **Provenance, checked 2026-08-20.** The entire "from Tipperary" lore traces to a single 2025 contact with **Robert Gleeson Sr.** (`research/results/gleeson-initial.md`). The **only verbatim quotation** preserved from him is:

    > *"Dad mentioned relatives from Wisconsin traveling to **Cork** to visit Gleeson relatives there"*

    **Cork — not Tipperary.** "Tipperary" appears nowhere in the quoted words; it survives only in a summarising bullet ("Extended family also lived in Tipperary and Cork") written by whoever took the note. Matt's own recollection of the exchange is that it was a single DM along the lines of *"dad remembers grandpa talking about family going back to Cork to visit, maybe Tipperary"* — i.e. **Tipperary was the hedge, Cork was the claim**, and it is third-hand (Robert's father's memory of his grandfather).

    ⚠️ **The same source produced at least two claims since disproven** — that Patrick was the "sailor at The Windmill, Limerick City" (contamination from an unrelated family, withdrawn), and that James may have married in Ireland (he married in Wisconsin in 1892). It is friendly recollection, not testimony.

    **So the Emly find and the lore must not be used to corroborate each other.** Treating a hedged, partly-discredited, third-hand DM as independent confirmation of a document — or the document as vindication of the DM — manufactures convergence out of one weak source and one unrelated fact. `docs/project_incoming/tree-2026-06-03.md`'s line that *"Robert Gleeson's tri-county lore turned out to be literal geography"* is exactly this error and should not be repeated.

    **Correct statement:** the family was resident in a Tipperary townland in 1833, three kilometres from the Limerick line. Nothing in the record speaks to a family tradition, and **Cork remains entirely unevidenced** — the Cork lead is already correctly parked.

    **But calibrate it — this is a three-kilometre Tipperary.** Ballinvreena lies roughly **3.4 km from Hospital village and 3.2 km from Emly village**, almost exactly on the county line. This is *not* a family that migrated from Tipperary into Limerick. It is a family living **on the boundary** that used whichever chapel was nearer at the time — which is why exactly one baptism out of nine lands in a Tipperary register while the rest are Hospital. (FamilySearch indexes only **four** Gleeson baptisms at Emly across 1820–45, so the family's appearance there is an outlier in that register, not a pattern.) **Do not over-tell the Tipperary story.**

    **Untested hint, recorded because it is cheap:** *Baile an Bhrianaigh* means O'Brien's town; the mother is Honora **O'Brien**; the 1833 sponsors are **Pat O'Brien** and Ellen Walsh; the 1842 sponsor is **Margaret O'Brien**. The couple may have been living on her people's ground before moving to Herbertstown by 1838. The placename is medieval and proves nothing on its own — the **Tithe Applotment book for Ballinvreena (~1826–34)** is the direct test.

**⭐ Malachy Gleeson of Herbertstown — a better handle than any James.** Patrick's brother **Malachy** was baptised 12 July 1835 and would have been about sixteen in 1851 — far too young to be a Griffith's occupier. **❌ James Gleeson, Patrick's father, held no land.** He is **not a Griffith's occupier anywhere in Hospital civil parish** — only Michaels and a Patrick appear. **❌ No Gleesons at all in Emly civil parish, Co. Tipperary** — so none at Ballinvreena. **❌ Kilfinny is empty of both Gleesons and Moloneys**, corroborating the 2026-06-09 finding that the Molony and Kelly families at Ballymackeamore were post-1851 in-migrants.

**Direction of the corridor.** Of **139 Moloney** and **95 Kenny** records in Co. Limerick, only **1** and **3** respectively fall anywhere in Patrick's home district. Against dozens of Gleeson households in the Croom cluster, that is negligible. **The pre-existing link ran Gleeson-westward — the Gleesons were the mobile party, not the Moloneys.**

Skagh and Boherygeela are striking because of *where* they are, not because the names prove anything. The corridor hypothesis survived a test it could have failed; it is not established.
