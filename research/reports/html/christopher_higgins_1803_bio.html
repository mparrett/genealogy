Christopher Higgins (1803 – after 1882)

Relationship: 4th great-grandfather (Higgins line)

Dublin-Born, Wicklow-Married, Father of the Generation That Scattered

Christopher Higgins was born on November 26, 1803, in Dublin and baptized that same day at St. Nicholas (Without) — a parish on the south side of the Liffey. His parents, Nicholas Higgins (1770–c. 1867) and Eleanora Smyth (1769–1811), had recently relocated the family from their ancestral seat at Baltyboys, in the Wicklow Mountains south of Dublin, to a Charlotte Street address in the city. Christopher was eight years old when his mother Eleanora died in June 1811.

On a date in August 1825, at age twenty-one, Christopher returned to the family's original parish to marry Bridget Darcy. The ceremony took place at Valleymount, County Wicklow — the parish of Blackditches/Boystown/Valleymount, where his grandparents John Higgins and Anne Thomas lay buried in Baltyboys Cemetery. Whether Bridget was a local Wicklow woman whose family Christopher had known through summer visits, or whether the choice of venue reflected sentiment rather than necessity, the record does not say.

The couple settled in the Dublin suburbs and raised at least seven children. James (b. ~1829, our direct ancestor) was followed by Mary Anne (1830), Thomas (b. 3 Jan 1833, Rathfarnham), Christopher (1834), Anne (b. 1 Dec 1837), Catherine (1838–1882), and Margaret (b. Nov 1840). The family moved through several Dublin addresses — Rathgar, Rathfarnham, and Harold's Cross (the latter documented in the 1845 Valuation Book).

Christopher's children would scatter across three continents. Mary Anne sailed for Australia and died at Kincumber, New South Wales in 1899. Christopher (1834) followed her there and died unmarried at the Oriental Hotel in Sydney in 1866. Thomas (1833) and his wife Bridget Brereton took their eight children to Jersey City, New Jersey, in 1871. Only James stayed in Dublin to raise his own large family — and James's son Laurence would in turn cross the Atlantic in the 1880s, joining his uncle Thomas's branch in the American Northeast.

Bridget Darcy died before 1854, leaving Christopher a widower. Christopher himself was still alive when his daughter Catherine died in 1882 at age 78 (or 44, depending on which Catherine the record refers to) — placing his own death sometime after that year. The exact date and place have not been documented.
