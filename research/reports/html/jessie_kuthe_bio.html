Jessie Velma Kuthe (1905–1993)

Relationship: Great-grandaunt (Kuthe line) — younger sister of Doris

Writer, Twice-Married, Keeper of the Family's Migration Story

Jessie Velma Kuthe was born on April 10, 1905, in West Albany, Linn County, Oregon, the fifth child of George Washington Kuthe (1869–1925) and Huldah Imogene Bridgefarmer (1873–1907). Like her older sister Doris, Jessie lost her mother early: Huldah died of tuberculosis on August 6, 1907, when Jessie was just two years old. The two sisters—Doris and Jessie—would remain close for the rest of their long lives, eventually sharing a single household into their old age.

The Journey South

Around 1911–1912, when Jessie was six or seven, the Kuthe family left Oregon for Phoenix, Arizona, joining the migration of families drawn to the growing Southwest. Jessie made the trip by train—and, remarkably, she wrote it down. Her first-person account of the family's railroad journey from Oregon to Arizona survives as a journal, passed down through the family: her nephew Howard kept it on the Higgins side, while a copy descended through her son Bill's children. It is the closest thing the family has to a contemporary record of the Kuthes' move west, told in the voice of the child who lived it.

Phoenix and the Rosser Marriage

Jessie grew up in Phoenix. On June 5, 1922, at the age of seventeen, she married Willis Andrew Rosser Sr. (1898–1969). Their son, Willis Andrew "Bill" Rosser Jr., was born on Christmas Day, 1923, in Phoenix.

Silver City and the Depression Years

By 1930 Jessie was living on her own in Silver City, New Mexico, at 903 West 12th Street, working for the Forest/Power office near the New Mexico teachers' college. The marriage to Willis was coming apart, and like her sister Doris—then a young single mother building a teaching career in Arizona—Jessie faced the hard arithmetic of raising a child through the Great Depression.

The two sisters arrived at a shared solution. By family account, during the early-to-mid 1930s Jessie's son Bill and Doris's son Howard were "sent in together" to St. Mary Magdalene's Academy, a Catholic boarding school run by the Sisters of St. Joseph in Silver City, while their mothers established themselves nearby. Jessie later removed Bill; Howard, after his own time in New Mexico, went to live with Robert and Sarah Birch at Agua Caliente, Arizona. (The institution was identified by a Silver City historian, who found some confirmation that Bill was lodged there; direct enrollment records for the boys have not yet been located, as the 1930 census lists only the school's nuns, not its students.)

San Francisco and a Second Marriage

By the late 1930s Jessie had moved to San Francisco, where she worked as a stenographer. By April 1940 the census found her as "Rosser, Divorced, Head" at 1020 Pine Street; the divorce decree itself has not been located. Around 1940 or early 1941 she married Roy William Adair (1889–1969). No marriage record has yet surfaced, but it is bracketed neatly: she was the divorced "Mrs. Rosser" in April 1940, and her daughter Imogene Anne "Juby" Adair was born under the Adair name on May 3, 1941, in Los Angeles. Through the 1940s the Adairs lived in the Pasadena and San Marino area of California.

Nogales and the Sisters' Household

Around 1950 Jessie returned to Arizona, settling in Nogales, Santa Cruz County, where she shared a Court Street household with her sister Doris for the rest of her life. Juby's own obituary recalled growing up on Court Street "with her mother, Jessie Adair, and aunt, Doris McGuire"—the 1950 census confirms it, listing Jessie as "Sister" in Doris's home. The bond formed when the two motherless girls came up together in Oregon and Arizona closed the circle in Nogales, more than four decades later.

A Writer's Voice

Jessie was, by family reputation and by the record, a writer. Beyond her childhood journal of the migration, she published a memoir essay in Ford Times in March 1959, portraying her girlhood home of Albany, Oregon as "a transplanted New England village." A local notice, "Jessie Kuthe Adair Tells Of Albany's Past" (Albany Democrat-Herald, March 21, 1959), described her as "the descendant of pioneers who came to the Willamette valley by covered wagon"—a fitting line for the family's chronicler.

Children

Jessie's two children both made notable lives. Bill Rosser served in World War II with the Eighth Air Force's 385th Bomb Squadron, surviving 50 missions over Germany and earning the Distinguished Flying Cross; he went on to earn bachelor's and master's degrees from Caltech and spent over forty years as a research scientist in the aerospace industry. Her daughter Juby, as Imogene Bell (m. George Graham Bell), became a pioneering nurse practitioner in Nogales.

Jessie died on March 27, 1993, in Nogales, Arizona, at the age of 87, and is buried there.
