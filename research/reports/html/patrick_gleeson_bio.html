Patrick Gleeson (c. 1832–1897)

Relationship: 3rd great-grandfather (Gleeson line)

Croom, County Limerick

Patrick Gleeson was born around 1832–1837 in County Limerick, Ireland. He was the son of James Gleeson and Honora O'Brien of Hospital parish, on the Limerick–Tipperary border — a family documented across four baptisms in the Hospital register (Patrick 1829, Patrick 1833, Margaret 1842, Catherine 1850). Two of those baptisms record a Patrick to this couple (March 1829 and St. Patrick's Day 1833); the weight of evidence — a calibrated birth estimate near 1832, a sponsor side-switch from the Gleeson side to the O'Brien side, and a death record giving his age as 60 in 1897 — points to the later child as ours, with the earlier Patrick likely having died young. As a young man, Patrick moved roughly 25 miles west to the townland of Scagh in Croom parish, where he first appears in the record in 1859. His occupation evolved over his life: he was a farmer through the 1860s (documented in Petty Sessions records of 1859, 1864, and 1869), a shopkeeper on Main Street in Croom village by 1881, and a carrier by the time of his death. The "sailor" attribution from earlier research was contamination from a separate Limerick City Patrick Gleeson family — see research/analysis/gleeson-three-family-confusion.md.

Croom, Co. Limerick
Ireland, c. 1862
The market town of Croom sits on the River Maigue in Co. Limerick. Patrick raised his family in the nearby townland of Scagh, where seven of his children were baptized between 1863 and 1878.

On 18 November 1860, Patrick married Margaret Moloney of Kilfinny in the Croagh/Kilfinny parish church. The marriage register records a dispensation for consanguinity in the third or fourth degree — Patrick and Margaret were blood relatives, the Gleeson and Moloney lines having already intermarried in an earlier generation. (The register names the bride "Maria," but three of her children's baptisms, the 1901 and 1911 censuses, an 1918 deed, and her 1922 death record all name her Margaret; "Maria" was a lone scribal slip.) Margaret was herself baptized 31 December 1836 in Croagh parish, daughter of James Molony and Catherine Kenny.

Margaret's surname appears in records under several spellings—Moloney, Molony, Malony, Maloney—but the Croom parish baptism records for the children consistently use the Moloney form (Latin Moloney). This is an important distinction: Ancestry trees that merge our line with a Maloney family are following the wrong surname and likely the wrong family entirely.

The couple had at least nine documented children. Their firstborn was Catherine, baptized at Croom in January 1862 — eldest of the family and, as later research established, the "Mrs. John Hayes of Staten Island" named in James's 1946 obituary (see below). Eight more children followed, baptized in Croom parish between 1863 and 1881:

  • Catherine Gleeson (baptized 31 Jan 1862) — firstborn; sponsors: John Moloney, Bridget Gleeson. Married John Hayes at Croom in 1879; died 1944 on Staten Island, New York
  • James (Jacobus) Gleeson (23 Dec 1863) — our direct ancestor; sponsors: William Storan, Margaret Kiely. Emigrated to Wisconsin c. 1883
  • Honora ("Nora") Gleeson (24 Mar 1866) — sponsors: Thomas Shea, Ellen Moloney
  • Bridget (Brigida) Gleeson (21–24 Feb 1868)
  • Ellen / Helena Gleeson (9 Jul 1869) — sponsors: Jeremiah O'Leary, Bridget Shanahan; officiant Rev. John Quinlan
  • Mary Gleeson (18 Dec 1872) — sponsors: John + Ellen O'Shaughnessy; officiant Rev. John Quinlan, C.C. Likely the "Miss Mary Gleeson, Croom" of James's obituary
  • Margaret (Margarita) Gleeson (29 Sep 1875) — sponsors: William Blake, Ellen Condon
  • Jane / Janet Gleeson (25–28 Sep 1878) — sponsors: Michael Moloney, Maria Quaid
  • Bridget Gleeson (born 11 Oct 1881, Croom) — a second Bridget; registered by Patrick himself as a shopkeeper. Almost certainly the "Mrs. Bridget O'Connor of Croom" of James's 1946 obituary

The baptismal sponsors—William Storan and Margaret Kiely for James—point to a local network of families in the Croom area. Storan is a distinctively Croom-area surname (Gaelic Ó Stóirín); Mark Storan appears in the 1820s Tithe Applotment Books for Lower Corrobal in Croom, and the Storan family has farmed in Mournane/Lower Morenane (Croom parish) continuously since. The recurring Moloney sponsors across the children's baptisms (John Moloney, Ellen Moloney, Michael Moloney) reflect Margaret's nearby kin.

James's 1946 obituary named three surviving sisters: Mrs. Bridget O'Connor and Miss Mary Gleeson in Croom, and Mrs. John Hayes on Staten Island — all three now identified among the documented children above.

Emigration of the Children

At least two of Patrick and Margaret's children crossed the Atlantic. Their firstborn Catherine married John Hayes at Croom on 29 November 1879 — the officiating priest, Rev. John Quinlan, was the same curate who baptized James, Mary, and Margaret, and the witnesses (Michael and Catharina Shanahan) had been married two months earlier with Patrick himself standing as a witness. Catherine and John emigrated to New York, settling on Staten Island. Catherine died there on 1 November 1944 at 64 Edison Street, a widow; her death record names her parents as Patrick and Margaret Gleason, confirming her as the "Mrs. John Hayes of Staten Island" of James's 1946 obituary. (An earlier hypothesis identifying "Mrs. John Hayes" as an Ella Isabel Gleason of an 1874 Eau Claire, Wisconsin marriage was withdrawn once that bride's father proved to be a "C. R. Gleason" — a different family entirely.)

James emigrated around 1883, arriving in New York from Liverpool and Queenstown at approximately age 19, and settled in Buffalo County, Wisconsin (the Mondovi area). Patrick and Margaret themselves remained in Ireland; no U.S. records exist for either.

In the Record

  • 11 September 1859 — Croom, Co. Limerick. Patrick was charged with being "Drunk on the Public Street at Croom" by Acting Constable Thomas Turner. Court appearance 19 September 1859 before Justices Robert Maxwell and C. J. Collins R.M. Occupation listed: Farmer. Residence on the record: "Sean" — almost certainly Scagh/Scaugh (same justice, same court, same charge pattern as the 1864 record). Fined 2 shillings 6 pence + 1 shilling costs, or 24 hours imprisonment. Source: National Archives of Ireland, Petty Sessions Order Books, CSPS1/8381. This is the earliest primary record of Patrick — 4 years before James's baptism — and the first documentary evidence of his occupation as a farmer.
  • 14 June 1864 — Croom, Co. Limerick. Patrick was charged again with being "Drunk on the Street at Croom" by Sub-Constable Oliver Goale (assisted by Sub-Constable Edmond Sullivan, witness Sub-Constable W. Eakins). Court appearance 18 July 1864 before Justices Robert Maxwell and Henry Lyons. Fined two shillings + one shilling costs, or 24 hours imprisonment. Residence on the record: "Scaugh" (= Scagh townland). Source: National Archives of Ireland, Petty Sessions Order Books, CSPS1/8382.
  • 3 November 1881 — Croom. Patrick registered the birth of his daughter Bridget, signing the form himself ("Patt Gleeson, Father") and recorded as a shopkeeper on Main Street — direct evidence that he was literate, alive, and had moved from farming in Scagh to keeping a shop in the village.

Death

Patrick died on 7 December 1897 at Croom, aged 60, after about a year of heart disease. His occupation at death was recorded as carrier, and his daughter Bridget Gleeson was present and reported the death, registered six days later. (The same Bridget's 1924 marriage record names her late father as "Patrick Gleeson, Farmer, Main Street Croom," independently confirming this as our Patrick.) The death at age 60 places his birth around 1837.

Margaret outlived him by a quarter-century. By the 1901 and 1911 censuses she was the widowed head of the household on Main Street, Croom, running the shop, with daughters Mary and Bridget at home; she signed her own census form in 1911 at age 66. She was still transacting business — releasing a mortgage — in January 1918. Margaret died at Croom on 18 May 1922, recorded as a 74-year-old widow (an understatement; a birth around 1836 would make her closer to 86). Her daughter Bridget was again the informant.

Disambiguation

Early research confused Patrick with an Australian Patrick Gleeson from the same era who married a Margaret Mahony. The two families were distinguished through several lines of evidence:

  • Our Patrick married Margaret Moloney (confirmed across multiple Croom baptism records for the children, and James's 1892 Wisconsin marriage record)
  • The Australian Patrick married Margaret Mahony (née Mahony, parents Patrick Mahony + Johanna O'Connell), who emigrated to Australia in 1856/7
  • The Australian Patrick had 11 children, none named James
  • Correspondence with a descendant of the Australian line (Tony) confirmed no DNA match

A separate spelling confusion exists between Moloney and Maloney—two distinct Irish surnames that are often conflated in U.S. records and Ancestry trees. Our line is Moloney, confirmed consistently across the Croom parish baptisms. Trees merging in a Maloney wife are likely importing the wrong family.

A third confused family is a Limerick City Patrick Gleeson, a sailor who lived at "The Windmill" in St Michael's District, Limerick City. He was married to Mary Mahony (not Margaret Moloney). Their son John was born 17 May 1864 in Limerick City—only five months after our James was baptized in Croom, which is biologically impossible if the same family. Earlier research wrongly imported this Limerick City family's sailor occupation and Windmill residence into our Patrick's profile; both have now been struck. See research/analysis/gleeson-three-family-confusion.md.

A fourth confused identification is Ella Isabel Gleason of the 24 Dec 1874 Eau Claire, WI marriage to John Nelson Hayes. The 1874 record names her father as C. R. Gleason (likely Charles, Chauncey, Cornelius, or similar) and the groom as John Nelson Hayes, born Venice, Cayuga County, NY, son of Henry and Elmira Hayes. John Nelson Hayes appears to have later partnered with Antoinette "Nettie" Ring, with children Ada (1881), Maud (1882), and Hugh (1889) in Dunn County, WI. None of this connects to our Croom Gleeson line. The misidentification — treating Ella as Patrick's eldest known daughter and "Mrs. John Hayes (NY)" in James's obit — was based on coincidental name overlap; it has been withdrawn 2026-06-01.
