Frederick Kuthe (c. 1822–1900)

Relationship: 3rd great-grandfather (Kuthe line)

Hesse
Germany, c. 1830
Frederick's family emigrated from Hesse, possibly near Darmstadt, arriving in Baltimore in 1834 when Frederick was a child. His father Andreas was 36 at the time of the voyage.

German Immigrant, Miller, Union Soldier

Frederick Kuthe (also recorded as George Frederick Kuthe) was born around 1827 in Darmstadt, Hesse, Germany.1 His father was Andreas Kuthe (born c. 1798 in Hesse). The 1870 federal census lists Frederick's birthplace as "Darmstadt," confirming the family tradition. A related Küthe family emigrated from Vöhl in the Waldeck region in 1845, but Darmstadt and Vöhl are roughly 150 km apart—the connection between the two families, if any, remains unclear.

A quarterly abstract of passenger arrivals for the district of Baltimore, covering April–June 1834, lists the Kuth family arriving from Germany: Andreas (age 36), his wife Anna D. (age 34), son Fredk (age 7), and daughter Margareth (age 3). Frederick came to America as a child, traveling with his family.2

An 1845 passenger manifest for the ship Olbers, sailing from Bremen to New Orleans, lists a Küthe family from Vöhl (in the Waldeck region of Hesse) headed by Heinrich Küthe (age 52), with an 18-year-old "Friederich" among his children. This was previously thought to be our Frederick, but the 1834 Baltimore manifest—which matches Andreas's known birth year and confirms the father-son relationship—indicates Frederick was already in the United States by 1845. The Olbers Friederich appears to be a cousin or other relative, not our ancestor.3 The two families may share a common origin—the overlapping names (Anna, Friedrich, Margareth) suggest kinship—but no direct link between Andreas and Heinrich has been established. A biography of Louis Schleicher, a baker in Lancaster, Ohio, notes that he married "Miss Mary C. Kuthe, who was born in Germany in 1824, and came to America in 1845"—Mary traveled on the Olbers with Heinrich's family and may be a relative of our Frederick, but the connection is unconfirmed.4

By the 1850s Frederick had settled in the Spring Creek township of Dent County, Missouri, which had been organized in 1851. He worked as a miller, not a farmer as previously assumed—both the 1870 census and his 1865 military enlistment record list his occupation as miller.5 The surname Kuthe proved difficult for English-speaking clerks, appearing in records variously as Kuethe, Küthe, Kuth, Kuthy, Koothe, and even "Kite."

In the final months of the Civil War, Frederick enlisted in the Union Army in New York City on February 14, 1865, at age 36. He was mustered into Company G, 9th New York Cavalry as a private.6 His enlistment record describes him as born in Germany, 5'6", with blue eyes and light hair, occupation miller. The 9th NY Cavalry saw action in the Shenandoah Valley and around Petersburg in the war's closing weeks. Frederick was mustered out on July 17, 1865, at Cloud's Mills, Virginia. Why he was in New York rather than Missouri at the time of enlistment is unknown—late-war bounty enlistments drew men from across the country to fill depleted units.

On January 19, 1868, Frederick married Sarah Ann Patterson (née Steagall) in Dent County.7 Sarah (born c. 1837) was a widow—her first husband had been a man named Patterson, and Steagall was her maiden name. The Dent County marriage register (Book A, 1851–1870) records the union of "KUTHE, GEORGE F." to "Sarah A. Patterson."8 Sarah brought three children from her first marriage: W. C. Paterson (b. c. 1859), N. E. Paterson (b. c. 1861), and J. H. Paterson (b. c. 1863)—all present in the 1870 census household.5 The couple settled near Salem, the county seat.

Frederick and Sarah's confirmed children include George Washington Kuthe (14 Oct 1869–1925) and Charles Fred Kuthe (31 Mar 1875). Charles Fred's WWI draft card lists "Geo. W. Kuthe" as his sibling, confirming the relationship.9 A Dent County history from the 1880s attributes "six living children: William, Mary (Mrs. Schmit), George, Catherine, Susan, Adam" to a Kuthe household, but this source may describe a different family and should be treated with caution.10 George was born in Salem and would later migrate to Oregon and then Arizona.

Frederick owned 100 acres across sections 34 and 35, Township 34, Range 6, in the Spring Creek area of Dent County.[^8b] Tragedy struck the family when Sarah Ann died in 1876 at approximately 39 years of age, leaving Frederick a widower with young children to raise.11 A delinquent tax notice in The Salem Monitor (June 15, 1876) lists "Kuthe Fred" owing $6.07 for 1875 taxes on this land — a sign of the strain on the household after Sarah's death. The 1876 Missouri state census confirms her death: Frederick appears as head of household (male, over 45) with sons George W. and Charles, stepdaughter Nancy Patterson (c. 15), and a Wm. Ray (possibly a boarder or hired hand). No wife is listed.

Frederick died sometime before 1883 — likely around 1879 — leaving George (age ~10) and Charles Fred (age ~4) orphaned.12 Hiram Hodges was appointed guardian and curator of the minor children, while Wm. C. Patterson — Sarah's eldest son from her first marriage — served as administrator of the estate.13 Patterson turned over $159.45 from the estate to Hodges for the children's care.14 By the 1880 census, Charles Fred was living with the Craig family in Current township, Dent County, listed as "brother-in-law" to head Kennedy Craig — Nancy Craig (19) was Nancy E. Patterson, Sarah's daughter from her first marriage, who had married Kennedy Craig and taken in her young half-brother. A final settlement notice appeared in The Salem Monitor on June 7, 1883, and the estate was formally closed September 1888.

Frederick Kuthe's life was cut short — arriving as a child in Baltimore, working as a miller in the Missouri Ozarks, serving briefly in the Union Army, and dying around age 52, just three years after his wife Sarah. He left behind orphaned children who were taken in by relatives and neighbors. Despite this, his sons carried the family westward: George to Oregon and then Arizona, Charles Fred to Oregon and then Washington. The family's German heritage persisted through the generations even as the surname shifted through a dozen spellings in American records.
