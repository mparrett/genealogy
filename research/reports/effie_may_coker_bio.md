# Effie May Coker (1878–1958)
**Relationship:** 2nd great-grandmother (Mowrey line)

*Four Marriages, Two States, One Resilient Life*

Effie May Coker was born on March 21, 1878, in Precinct 5, Red River County, Texas, the eldest daughter of **James A. Coker** (1855–1891) and **Amanda W. Calhoun** (1853–1944). She had four siblings who survived to adulthood: Nellie (1880), Willie Ann (1883), James Calhoun (1888), and Norman Wesley (1891). A curious note in the 1880 census records two-year-old "Effa" as blind—whether this reflected a childhood condition she outgrew or a census error is unknown.

Effie's father James was a carpenter in Red River County. He died on May 6, 1891, when Effie was thirteen, leaving Amanda to raise five children alone. Amanda would prove as resilient as her daughter—she lived to ninety-one, dying in Paris, Texas, in 1944.

At sixteen, Effie married **Joseph Hambrock** (1876–1900) on June 14, 1894, in Lamar County. Their daughter **Cecilia Mae Hambrock** was born in March 1895 in Paris, Texas. Joseph died young, around 1900, leaving Effie a widow for the first time at twenty-two.

On June 12, 1897, Effie married **Samuel S. Mowrey** (1867–1904) in Lamar County. Their children came quickly: **Frank Preston Mowrey** in December 1898, **Raymond Robert Mowrey** in December 1899, and **Ruby Mowrey** in April 1902. The 1900 census shows the young family in Justice Precinct 1, Lamar County, Samuel farming, Effie keeping house with a growing brood.

Then came the catastrophe of August 29, 1904. Samuel died at thirty-seven—on the same day as his father Moses Mansfield Mowery. Effie was twenty-six, with four children under ten.

She did not stay single long. In 1907, she married **Hedge B. Evans** (1881–1949). By the 1910 census she was living in Paris Ward 4, listed as a dressmaker—a trade that suggests she was working to support her family. This marriage too would end, though the circumstances are unclear.

On August 9, 1911, in Sebastian County, Arkansas, Effie married for the fourth and final time: **Andrew Montgomery Dawson** (1879–1938), a Texan born in Hailsboro. Two more daughters followed: **Loyce Maxine Dawson** in July 1913 (born in Oklahoma) and **Thelma Dawson** in August 1915 (born in Bogata, Red River County, Texas). The Oklahoma birth and the Fort Smith, Arkansas, marriage suggest a period of movement across state lines before the family settled.

By 1920, the Dawsons had made their way to **Williams, Arizona**, a railroad town on the future Route 66. Andrew worked as a hostler, tending railroad horses and equipment, and later became the town's head electrician. The family put down roots—voter registrations, city directories, and census records place them in Williams and Coconino County through the 1930s. Her son Raymond, who had followed her to Arizona, was building his own life in Williams, eventually becoming Rotary Club president and operator of the Sultana Bar.

Andrew Dawson died on February 12, 1938, in Winslow, Arizona, at fifty-eight. He was buried there. Effie was widowed for the third time, now sixty years old.

The 1940 census finds Effie in Superior, Coconino County, listed as "mother-in-law" in someone's household—likely one of her daughters. By 1950 she was in Maricopa County, and by 1952 in Phoenix, at 1648 E. Solano Drive. Her son Frank Preston Mowrey's WWII draft card lists his mother as "Mrs. A. M. Dawson"—the name she carried for the last three decades of her life.

Effie May Coker Hambrock Mowrey Evans Dawson died on January 24, 1958, in Phoenix, at seventy-nine. Her obituary in the *Arizona Republic* ran under the name **Effie M. Dawson**. She was buried in Phoenix, Maricopa County.

Four marriages, widowed three times, mother to at least eight children across three husbands, Effie's life spanned from Reconstruction-era Texas to Eisenhower-era Arizona. Through it all, she kept her family together and moving forward.
