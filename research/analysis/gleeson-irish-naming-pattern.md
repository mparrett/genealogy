# Applying Irish naming patterns to Patrick + Margaret's family

**Date:** 2026-06-02
**Purpose:** Use the traditional Irish Catholic naming convention to infer Patrick Gleeson's parents (and Margaret Moloney's mother) from the names of their seven documented children. These inferences are predictive — they direct where to search for the next generation back.

---

## The traditional naming pattern (Irish Catholic, mid-19th century)

This was a strongly-held convention in Irish Catholic families of this era. Adherence was high but not absolute — some families broke pattern, and child mortality often shifted birth-order slots.

### For sons

| Birth order | Named after |
|---|---|
| 1st son | Father's father (paternal grandfather) |
| 2nd son | Mother's father (maternal grandfather) |
| 3rd son | Father |
| 4th son | Father's eldest brother |
| 5th son | Mother's eldest brother |

### For daughters

| Birth order | Named after |
|---|---|
| 1st daughter | Mother's mother (maternal grandmother) |
| 2nd daughter | Father's mother (paternal grandmother) |
| 3rd daughter | Mother |
| 4th daughter | Father's eldest sister |
| 5th daughter | Mother's eldest sister |

## Strongest inferences (high confidence)

### 1. Patrick's father is **James Gleeson**

The 1st-son rule is the most reliable in Irish Catholic naming. Our Patrick's eldest known son is James, so Patrick's father was almost certainly James Gleeson.

This squares with our 1864 Petty Sessions record (Patrick alive in Croom) plus the Croom-area Gleeson cluster we've seen in parish records — multiple "James/Jacobus Gleeson" appearances in Croom marriage and baptism witness lists during 1853–1864 are consistent with a James Gleeson generation in the area.

### 2. Patrick's mother is **Bridget [unknown maiden]**

The 2nd-daughter rule is nearly as reliable. Our 2nd daughter is Bridget (Brigida), so Patrick's mother was Bridget.

**This reinforces our existing hypothesis** that *Bridget Gleeson + Thomas Shea* (married Croom Aug 1864) is Patrick's **sister**. An Irish sister named Bridget who married locally and stayed in Croom would be exactly what a "named-after-mother Bridget" generation pattern produces. See `gleeson-croom-family.md` for the Bridget+Shea evidence trail.

### 3. Margaret Moloney's mother is **Honora**

The 1st-daughter rule. Our 1st daughter is Honora, so Margaret Moloney's mother was Honora.

This is a new piece of information about Margaret's birth family. Combined with the Moloney brothers (Patrick, Michael, John) we've identified in Croom-area records, the Moloney parents would be **[Patrick/James/John/Michael?] Moloney + Honora [maiden]**.

## Lower-confidence inferences

### 4. Patrick's eldest sister might be Mary

The 4th-daughter rule suggests Mary. We have a "Miss Mary Gleeson, Croom" in James's 1946 obituary — but that's Patrick's daughter, not sister. There could be a separate Mary Gleeson generation aunt.

### 5. Margaret's eldest sister might be Jane

The Jane (6th daughter, 1878) slot doesn't have a clean match in the pattern. Could be Margaret's eldest sister, or could be Patrick's 2nd eldest sister, or just a chosen name.

---

## The broken 3rd-daughter rule

Our 3rd daughter is Ellen (1869), not Margaret (mother's own name as the pattern requires). Three explanations, in order of plausibility:

1. **An earlier daughter Margaret died.** A pre-1866 Margaret who didn't survive infancy would have taken the slot, leaving Ellen for 3rd-surviving. Check Croom register pre-1866 for an earlier Margaret baptism that doesn't reappear.
2. **The mother's name was actually Helen/Ellen.** Some Catholic women carried double-names (Margaret Ellen, or Latin Helena = vernacular Ellen). The Croom register's Latin "Margaritæ" might be a formalized version of a colloquial Helen/Ellen. Less likely given the consistent Margarita/Margaret across all 7 children's baptism records.
3. **The family simply broke pattern.** Happens, especially in families with strong personal preferences or godparent influence. Less analytically useful.

The **first explanation** is the most genealogically interesting — it predicts an undocumented earlier child.

## Limitations

- The pattern depends on knowing **birth order** accurately. If we have James as the firstborn son but there was an earlier deceased son, the pattern shifts.
- Parental name traditions sometimes used **second names** or **anglicized vs. Gaelic forms** — Honora ≈ Nora ≈ Annie; Margaret ≈ Madge; Ellen ≈ Helen ≈ Helena.
- A family might **deliberately break pattern** if a child was born after a death anniversary or to honor a godparent.
- The pattern is **predictive, not documentary** — these are hypotheses to verify, not facts.
