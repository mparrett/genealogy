## Headline

**Nothing found today weakens Catherine's core identification, and one secondary claim gets stronger while another needs its wording softened.**

The Croom marriage register contains exactly one Patrick Gleeson across the whole twenty years from October 1864 to the register's close in June 1884. No competing Patrick exists to be confused with Catherine's father. But the entry itself is bare — a name and nothing else — so the identification rests on the absence of a rival plus the surrounding family association, not on anything internal to the record. That supports "consistent with", not "conclusive".

## Recommended wording changes in `research/findings/patrick-gleeson/facts.md`

### 1. Downgrade — the reciprocal witness claim

**Current:** *"Patrick Gleeson as reciprocal witness 22 Sept 1879 … Kin-friend witness pattern **conclusive**."*

**Recommended:** *"…Kin-friend witness pattern is **strong contextual evidence** and no competing Patrick Gleeson appears in the Croom marriage register 1864–1884; the entry itself gives no residence, occupation or relationship, so it corroborates rather than proves."*

**Why:** the sweep found no second Patrick, which is the good outcome — but the plan's own decision rule for that outcome explicitly stops short of proof. Separately, the argument is now better supported than "conclusive" implied, because the Gleeson–Shanahan tie turns out to run back a decade (see 3 below).

### 2. Correction — the 1879 Shanahan bride's surname

**Current:** *"Michael Shanahan + Catherine **Lynch**'s wedding"*.

**On the image:** the bride's surname begins with a clear **S**, reading **Sy—** and most likely **Synan**; the line runs off the edge of the crop taken. It is certainly not Lynch.

**Recommended:** re-read frame 161 at the right-hand margin and correct. Until then, mark the surname **uncertain**. This does not affect the argument — the groom Michael Shanahan and his wife Catherine Shanahan are the pair who witness Catherine's own marriage two months later, and that is unaffected by the bride's maiden name.

### 3. Upgrade — the Gleeson–Shanahan association

**New evidence, not previously in `facts.md`:**

- **19 June 1869** — **Anna Gleeson** is a witness at the marriage of **Jacobus Shanahan**.
- **4 August 1869** — **Patrick Shanahan** is a witness at the marriage of **Margaret Gleeson**.

**Recommended:** add these, and reframe the 1879 exchange as *the late instance of a standing Gleeson–Shanahan relationship running from at least 1869*, rather than as a self-standing coincidence. This is the single biggest gain of the session for the identification argument, because it no longer depends on the forename Patrick being unique.

### 4. Correction — Margaret's birth date, and retire the twin question

**Current:** Margaret is recorded by her baptism, 29 September 1875, and the plan raised a possible twin relationship to Nora.

**New:** civil birth registration, Croom page 200 entry 463 — **born 27 September 1875 at Skagh**, father Patrick Gleeson, farmer, mother Margaret Gleeson formerly Moloney, informant **"Patt Gleeson, Father"**, registered 2 November 1875.

**Recommended:** record the birth date as established, note the two-day birth-to-baptism interval, and mark the twin hypothesis **contradicted** — a twin born January or February 1876 is impossible against a 27 September 1875 birth.

**Recommended:** record the elimination, and add **Michael Gleeson & Mary McNamara of Fanningstown** to the Croom-area Gleeson cluster list as a distinct household alongside the Fedamore and Boherygeela clusters already catalogued.

### 7. Upgrade — the 1862 sponsor is identified

**Current:** *"unidentified Gleeson kinswoman, possibly his sister"*.

**New:** the 1864 civil marriage certificate was obtained (Croom 1864, entry 23). **Bridget Gleeson, resident at Scagh, daughter of James Gleeson, farmer**, married Thomas O'Shea of Croom on **27 August 1864**, witnesses William Storan and Ellen Moloney, celebrant John Quinlan P.P. Both parties signed their own names.

**Recommended:** she is no longer unidentified. Wording: **the 1862 godmother and the 1864 bride are the same woman — probable, bordering on established**, resting on the matching townland (Scagh) and the matching associate circle.

**But do NOT write "sister".** The subsequent Hospital sweep (see `research/results/gleeson-bridget-hospital-baptism-search.md`) found **no baptism for a Bridget Gleeson to James Gleeson and Honora O'Brien anywhere between January 1836 and December 1844**, and turned up **at least four distinct James Gleeson couples in Hospital parish**, one of them living at Herbertstown itself. The father's-name match on the 1864 certificate is therefore **weak**, not strong. Correct wording: **a Gleeson kinswoman of Patrick's, of unestablished degree** — real relationship, unknown degree.


### 8. Correction — Patrick's 1833 baptism is at EMLY (the sponsor reading stands as it was)

**Read at image level:** NLI **Emly** parish, microfilm 02500/01 (**vtls000632708**), frame **75**, left page, March 1833:

> **17 — Patrick of James Gleeson & Honora O'Brien — Sps. Pat^k O'Brien & Ell[en] Walsh — Ballinvriana**

**One correction, and one non-correction that is worth recording because it was nearly made in error.**

**(a) Wrong parish — this one is real.** `facts.md` cites "Hospital parish page 94, Ballinvriana townland". The entry is in the **Emly** register (Co. Tipperary), not Hospital. The townland *Ballinvriana* and the date *17 March 1833* are correct as recorded. This confirms the family was baptising children on both sides of the Limerick–Tipperary line, and it is the documentary basis for the family's own "from Tipperary" tradition — which had until now rested on anecdote.

**(b) The sponsor reading in `facts.md` is CORRECT — do not change it.** The sponsors are **Pat^k O'Brien and Ellen Walsh**, exactly as `facts.md` and `assets/external/misc/patk-honora.md` have them. The maternal-grandfather inference built on it stands.

This is recorded because the reading was briefly and wrongly challenged during this session on the basis of an over-magnified crop, and the error is instructive:

- The NLI scan is **1500 px** across a two-page spread. Enlarging a single word to ~13× is reading JPEG ringing, not ink. Anything asserted at that magnification is unreliable.
- The correct test is **comparison against known exemplars in the same hand**. On this page the scribe writes *Patrick* in full four times; the capital **P** is a tall ascending loop that crests leftward and closes its bowl against the stem. His capital **C** — *Corbally*, *Carroll*, on the same page — is a single open curve with no closed ascender. The disputed initial has the **P** construction.
- Convention corroborates: **Pat^k** is the standard contraction for Patrick in Irish registers; **Cat^k** is not a form used for Catherine, and this scribe writes *Cath^ne* every time he means her (Cath^ne Quin, Cath^ne Burke, Cath^ne Gubbins, Cath^ne Carey — all on this page).

**Rule for the rest of this project:** before correcting an existing transcription on a letterform, find the same letter written unambiguously elsewhere in the same hand and compare. Do not correct from magnification alone.

### 9. Downgrade — the 1946 obituary "locks" nothing; it anchors

**Current:** `facts.md` says James's obituary **locks** the identification of Catherine.

**The tension:** James J. Gleeson's obituary (*St. Cloud Times*, 28 January 1946, p. 5) names **"Mrs. John Hayes, Staten Island, New York"** among his ***surviving*** sisters. Catherine died **1 November 1944** — fifteen months earlier.

**Assessment:** this is almost certainly stale family knowledge rather than a misidentification. The informant was most likely the daughter James lived with in Waite Park, Minnesota; a sister-in-law's death in New York could easily not have reached that household, and obituaries routinely list the already-dead as surviving. The obit's load-bearing contribution is unaffected — it places **a Croom-born Gleeson sister on Staten Island married to a John Hayes**, and whether she was still living at press time is irrelevant to that.

**Recommended:**

- Replace "**locks** the identification" with "**anchors** the identification".
- Record the fifteen-month discrepancy explicitly rather than leaving it to be rediscovered as an apparent contradiction.
- Note the consequence for the *other* names: the sister-list reflects **what Minnesota knew in January 1946**, so "Mrs. Brigit O'Connor" and "Miss Mary Gleeson" being alive at that date is softer evidence than it reads. Do not use the obit alone as a death-date floor for either.

## A methodological note for whoever picks this up

The Croom **civil registration district** is wider than the Croom **Catholic parish**. Several Gleeson marriages indexed in Croom civil district for 1864–1880 — Edmond Gleeson & Mary Griffin, 15 May 1869; Thomas Connelly & Bridget Gleeson, 4 Feb 1868; Patrick Toomey & Elizabeth Gleeson, 25 July 1868 — do **not** appear in the Croom Catholic marriage register frames covering those dates, which were read entry by entry. They were presumably celebrated in other Catholic parishes inside the same civil district. The two sources cannot be cross-validated one-to-one, and an absence from one is not an absence from the other.
