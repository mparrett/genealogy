## Family context

We are researching the **Higgins / Knight family** that lived in Manhattan and Newark, NJ from the 1880s through the 1930s. The principals:

- **Laurence Higgins** (b. 8 Jan 1854, Rathmines, Dublin, Ireland; d. before 17 Feb 1930, exact date/place unknown). Irish immigrant. Married twice. First wife **Mary Verner** died at the South Dublin Union workhouse 4 Oct 1881; surviving daughters from that marriage were Margaret Ester "Maggie" (1874) and Anne (1878, fate unknown). Second marriage to **Mary Knight**: per family records, ceremonies on 25 Nov 1883 (Dublin) and 6 Feb 1887 (Manhattan) — the 1887 ceremony is sourced (FamilySearch ARK 1:1:2445-BY5); the 1883 ceremony is **asserted but not independently sourced**.
- **Mary Knight Higgins** (b. ca. 1863 or 1866, Ireland; d. 27 Mar 1930, NYC Cancer Institute, Manhattan, transferred from a NYC almshouse where she was admitted 17 Feb 1930 as "widowed"). Per her 1930 NYC death certificate, parents were **James Knight + Bridget Larney**. Family-friend research treats her Ireland birth as settled — do not relitigate that.
- **Children of Laurence + Mary Knight:** Margaret (asserted c. 1882, possibly a tree-stitching artifact), Mary A. (1888–1913, m. Hamm, d. St. Joseph's Hospital, Bronx), Lawrence (b./d. 1890, infant, 12 Clarkson St., Manhattan), **James Everett** (1892–1938, our direct ancestor), Theresa Cecilia (1894–1980, lived institutionally at St. Agatha's / Clarkstown 1905–1915).
- **Children of Laurence + Mary Verner (Dublin, first marriage):** Margaret Ester "Maggie" (1874–1912, emigrated US, m. William J. Marshall, d. Passaic NJ), Mary Jane (1876–1879), **Anne (1878, fate unknown)**, twins Bridget and Mary Jane (b. 7 Sep 1881 at South Dublin Union, both likely died infant).

### Two source records anchor most current claims

1. **Mary Knight's 1930 NYC death certificate** — gives parents as James Knight + Bridget Larney (found by family genealogist Oct 2024 via NYC's a860-historicalvitalrecords portal; image not yet re-examined in detail).
2. **James Everett Higgins's 1906 New York Catholic Protectory reception card** (Reception No. 40130, State Board No. 19739; full transcription available on request) — documents an Aug 1906 family collapse: **father absconded**, **mother in the insane asylum on Ward's Island**, child living at **65 Downing Street, Manhattan** with **aunt Mrs. Quigley**; prior placement at St. Agatha's Home, Nanuet, for four years; subsequent aftercare at St. Philip's Home (417 Broome St.) and boarding at 1192 First Ave. c/o Kiernan in 1910.

### What we don't know (priority order)

1. **The Sligo origin attributed to Mary Knight.** The surname "Larney" is not documented in Sligo per John Grenham's *Irish Ancestors* index. A separate Theresa-line descendant (Brittany Bepler) records the family origin as "Sligo, surname Courtney" — a different mother's surname, same county. Smells like conflicting family hand-me-downs.
2. **The 1880 US Census attribution of the Knight family to Newark, NJ.** Asserted but the household enumeration has never been captured: no head, no address, no roster, no birthplaces, nothing.
3. **Laurence's actual US occupation history.** Bios say "porter (1900, 1910), janitor (1920)" — asserted from federal censuses but the enumerations are not in hand. The only directly-sourced US occupation is **longshoreman in 1890** (from infant Lawrence's 1890 death registration at 12 Clarkson, listing father's occupation as "L shore").
4. **Mary Knight's birth year:** 1866 (our records) vs 1863 (Mary Anne researcher's tree). Both unsourced.
5. **Margaret Higgins c. 1882** as a child of Laurence + Mary Knight — possibly real, possibly a tree-stitching artifact. Mary Anne researcher's tree omits her.
6. **The 25 Nov 1883 Dublin first ceremony** — asserted by us but not in Mary Anne's tree; she explicitly cannot find it.
7. **Laurence's immigration year, naturalization status, death date and place.**
8. **The duplicate-1879 sibling problem:** our records list Mary Knight's siblings as James J. (1867–1921), David H. (b. 1870), Mary Rebertha (1879–1902), and Elizabeth (b. 1879) — two daughters born the same year suggests either twins or a tree duplicate.
9. **Mrs. Quigley at 65 Downing Street, Manhattan** (named in 1906 as James's aunt) — working hypothesis: a paternal aunt by marriage (Higgins sibling who married a Quigley).
10. **Anne Higgins (b. 1878, Mary Verner's daughter)** — fate unknown.

## Secondary objectives — high value, may be partially online

### S1. Mary Knight's 1930 NYC death certificate (image, not just the indexed transcript)
- Already found via NYC database Oct 2024; image not yet examined.
- Source: NYC Department of Records, a860-historicalvitalrecords.nyc.gov (free, image-level access).
- Yields: exact venue, cause of death, residence at death, informant, parents' names and **birthplaces** (often Irish county for foreign-born). This is the single record most likely to resolve "Sligo vs not-Sligo."

### S2. Bridget Larney Knight's death record, Newark, 1894
- Asserted death year from Ancestry data; record not retrieved.
- Source: NJ State Archives index; FamilySearch NJ Deaths and Burials 1798–1971; Newark city archives.
- Yields: exact date, residence, cause, parents (if recorded), informant, birthplace.

### S3. 1887 NYC marriage record image (Laurence + Mary Knight)
- Source: FamilySearch ARK 1:1:2445-BY5 (already located).
- Re-examine for any field we may have missed beyond "Mary's parents not recorded": witnesses' addresses, ages, occupations; the church/celebrant name; Mary's stated residence.

### S4. 1910 and 1920 US Federal Census enumerations for the Higgins household
- Source: FamilySearch / Ancestry, both indexed.
- Yields: corroboration / contradiction of porter→janitor chain; confirms Mary's return from Ward's Island by 1910; documents household composition; in 1920, "years married" reaffirms or shifts the 1883 vs 1887 question.

### S5. NJ State Censuses 1885, 1895, 1905, 1915 — Higgins and Knight households
- NJ ran state censuses in years between federal censuses. All exist on FamilySearch (some images, some indexes only).

### S6. James J. Knight (1867–1921) life records
- Mary's brother; second-most-promising independent corroborator of Knight parents and origin after Mary's own death cert.
- Source: NJ death records; Newspapers.com obituaries (or free portions); Find a Grave.

### S7. Newark city directories (Trow's, R.L. Polk) 1880–1920
- Source: archive.org has many digitized directories for this era; Newark Public Library may have more behind a paywall.
- Yields: year-by-year residence + occupation tracking for both Laurence Higgins and James Knight; useful for confirming/refuting the porter chain and for fixing the 1880 Knight household address.

### S8. Mary Rebertha Knight death record (1902, location unknown)
- Resolves whether Mary Rebertha and Elizabeth are twins, duplicates, or distinct sisters with one dying young.
- Source: NJ vital records 1902; FamilySearch.

### S9. NYC immigration records for any Knight
- Castle Garden (pre-1892) and Ellis Island (post-1892) passenger lists.
- Searches: Knight family arriving NY 1860–1880 with a James + Bridget + several young children.
- Free at libertyellisfoundation.org and FamilySearch.

### S10. Thomas Higgins (1833 Rathfarnham → 1871 Jersey City) household — the American beachhead

**Why this matters:** Thomas is Laurence's paternal uncle (brother of James Higgins 1830). He emigrated to Jersey City in 1871, fifteen years before Laurence's own arrival, and is the documented American Higgins contact who likely explains why Laurence + Mary landed in the NY/NJ corridor. His one daughter is also a credible candidate for the **Mrs. Quigley** lead (1906 Protectory card, 65 Downing St., Manhattan), and his eldest son **Christopher b. 1856** is the working candidate for the 1887 marriage witness — but the entire witness identification rests on naming tradition + geographic plausibility with no baptismal record cited.

**What family genealogist Mary Anne Higgins has already documented** (don't re-walk):
- Thomas was originally bound for Australia under sponsorship by his sister Mary Anne's husband John Woodward (1859–60), but changed course.
- 1871 emigration to USA with wife **Bridget Brereton** (who appears as "Mary" Brereton in the 1880 US Census; her emigration manifest and death record give Bridget).
- The 1871 passenger manifest erroneously lists origin as **England** — likely from a brief Liverpool stopover.
- **9 children total: 1 daughter, 8 sons.** Last child born in the USA after arrival.
- 1880 US Census places the family in Jersey City — the firmest record.
- **Trail goes cold after 1880** — Mary Anne has tried and failed to locate them in 1900.
- Only one of the nine children fully traced: **Michael Higgins (1865–1924)** — DNA-anchored via descendants.
- US DNA match **Deborah Thompson (née Higgins)** descends from the Thomas branch (4th cousin to Mary Anne).
- Mary Anne speculates Thomas may have been alive into the 1890s — possibly arranging infant Lawrence Higgins's 1890 burial reroute from St. Peter's to Holy Name Cemetery, Jersey City.

**Gap-filling research targets** (where the agent can add value):
1. **The 1880 Jersey City enumeration of Thomas's household — full roster.** We have it placed there but no captured detail. We need every name, age, birthplace, occupation. The eldest son's name + age is the linchpin for the Christopher-1856-as-1887-witness theory: if a Christopher age ~24 appears at the top of the children list, the theory holds. If not, the witness theory needs rebuilding.
2. **The daughter's identity** — single most valuable gap. If she married a Quigley, the 1906 "aunt Mrs. Quigley" question collapses to her. Search NJ/NYC marriage indexes 1875–1905 for a Higgins-bride daughter of Thomas + Bridget/Mary marrying any surname, with priority on Quigley.
3. **Christopher b. 1856 — an independent baptismal or vital record.** Rathmines / Rathfarnham parish registers 1856–1858. Would convert the witness theory from [Provisional] to [Likely].
4. **NJ State Census 1885 / 1895** placements — these were not on Mary Anne's radar and may catch the family between federal years.
5. **Newark / Jersey City directories 1880–1900** — should yield year-over-year residence and occupation for Thomas.
6. **Holy Name Cemetery (Jersey City) records** — if the 1890 infant Lawrence reroute *was* arranged by Thomas, the plot/family records there may reveal him.
7. **Thomas's and Bridget's death records** — NJ Death Index 1885–1910. Bridget should be findable as either Bridget or Mary Higgins.

## Evidence standards

Please rate each finding with one of:
- **[Confirmed]** — primary source image obtained and the relevant fields verified.
- **[Likely]** — primary source transcript indexed; image not directly examined but content matches the predicted profile.
- **[Provisional]** — secondary source (public family tree, derivative compilation) — useful but treat with caution.
- **[Speculative]** — informed inference or single-source claim without independent corroboration.
- **[Conflict]** — finding contradicts a claim listed above; surface explicitly with both sides.

Prefer record-level URLs (FamilySearch ARK, FindAGrave memorial #, NYC Vital Records image link) over collection-level pointers. When citing a public Ancestry tree, name the tree owner and note the rating as [Provisional] at best.
