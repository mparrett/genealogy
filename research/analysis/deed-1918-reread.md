# The 1918 Molony → Gleeson deed — re-read against all four transcriptions

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Why:** the deed is one of the three remaining legs of the "1840 Margaret" hypothesis (`margaret-moloney-two-candidates.md`), and Matt flagged that the transcriptions were made by an earlier agent and may carry errors. They do.

**Sources on disk:** `assets/external/transcripts-memories-gleeson-molony-1918.md`, `-fs.md`, `-chat.md`, `-alt.md`

---

## ⚠️ First problem: one of the four files is a completely different deed

**`transcripts-memories-gleeson-molony-1918-alt.md` has nothing to do with this family.** It is a transcription of an **1884 Dublin conveyance** — Margaret Moloney of 47 North William Street, Dublin, widow, assigning 37 North Strand Road to Margaret Bartley of Seville Place — bundled with unrelated judgment affidavits from Tyrone and Donegal.

It shares only the surname *Moloney* and a widow named *Margaret*. The filename implies it is a variant transcription of the 1918 memorial. **It is not, and anyone reading it as corroboration would be badly misled.** It should be renamed or removed.

---

## What the three genuine transcriptions agree on

All three (`-fs`, `-chat`, and the base file) independently give the same substance:

> **A Memorial of an Indenture of Release made the tenth day of January 1918, between JEREMIAH MOLONY of Ballymacamore, Croom, in the County of Limerick, FARMER, of the one part, and MARGARET GLEESON of Main Street, Croom, in the County of Limerick, WIDOW, of the other part.**
>
> Whereby, after reciting as therein, it was witnessed that for the considerations therein **the said Jeremiah Molony as MORTGAGEE did thereby grant unto the said Margaret Gleeson her heirs and assigns** all the lands, hereditaments and premises expressed to be granted by the therein-recited **Indenture of Mortgage of the thirteenth day of June 1911** — viz. a **dwelling house with offices at the rear thereof, situate in the Main Street of Croom, and the strip of ground extending to the River Maigue**, being part of the lands of [*see note*] with the gardens of Croom and [*see note*], situate in the Parish of Croom, **Barony of Coshma**, County of Limerick.
>
> **To hold** unto the use of the said Margaret Gleeson, her heirs and assigns, **in fee simple, discharged from all principal money and interest secured by said Indenture.**
>
> Witnessed by **J. F. Cronin, Manager, Munster and Leinster Bank Limited, Croom**, and **P. G. Potter, Accountant, Munster and Leinster Bank Limited, Croom**. Sworn at Croom, 11 January 1918, before **William H. Lyons, J.P.** for Co. Limerick.

---

## ⭐⭐ The headline: the deed states NO relationship whatsoever

**Searched all three transcriptions for *brother, sister, cousin, nephew, niece, uncle, aunt*. Not one occurrence.**

Jeremiah is described only as **"farmer"** and **"as Mortgagee"**. Margaret only as **"Widow"**. This is a **purely commercial instrument** — a mortgage discharge. It carries no kinship language at all.

### And the witnesses point the same way

Both subscribing witnesses are officers of the **Munster and Leinster Bank, Croom branch** — the **Manager** and the **Accountant**. The release was executed at the bank.

That is how a **commercial** mortgage is discharged. A family arrangement between siblings or cousins would more typically be witnessed by a solicitor or by family. It does not *exclude* kinship — a well-off farming relative lending to a widow is an entirely normal rural Irish arrangement — but **nothing in the instrument or its execution suggests it.**

## ⚠️⚠️ A false citation in `catherine-gleeson-hayes/facts.md`

That file currently reads:

> *"the Ballymackeamore townland matches the 1918 Jeremiah Molony deed **identifying him as Margaret's brother**"*

**The deed does not identify him as anything of the kind.** It calls him a farmer and a mortgagee. This is a claim the cited source does not support.

Worse, it was **already retracted elsewhere**: the 2026-06-09 reconciliation in `patrick-gleeson/facts.md` established that Jeremiah was baptised to **Michael Molony and Honora Minihan**, concluded *"Jeremiah is NOT Margaret's brother, and Kate is NOT Margaret's niece,"* and struck through the old wording there. **The Catherine file was never updated**, so the retracted claim survived in a second location for over two months — and it is load-bearing for the "Helen Ryan = maternal first cousin" reading printed alongside it.

This is the same failure mode Matt identified in the Tipperary lore: **a summary drifting from what the source actually says, then being cited as if it were the source.** Corrected 2026-08-20.

## Effect on the "1840 Margaret" hypothesis

The deed was listed as **leg 2** of candidate B — *"the 1918 Jeremiah→Margaret mortgage release, which B explains and A leaves unexplained."*

**That leg is now much weaker.** The transaction is a mortgage discharge between a creditor and a debtor, executed at a bank, with no relationship stated. Candidate A explains it perfectly well: a Croom-area widow borrowed against her Main Street property in 1911 and redeemed it in 1918, from a farmer who had money to lend.

**Running total on candidate B:** leg 1 (the 1881 godparenthood) fell to the shopkeeper-neighbour reading; leg 2 (this deed) falls to the commercial reading. **Two of four legs gone in one session.** What remains is Helen Ryan as "Cousin" in 1944, and the 1860 witness "Michaele Moloney" — and the latter now has a *better* explanation in Margaret's own documented brother Michael (b. 13 Dec 1840).

⚠️ This does **not** disprove B. It removes the props that made it look strong. The falsifying test — finding whom the 1840 Margaret actually married — is still the thing that would settle it.

## What the deed genuinely does give us

1. **Margaret was a widow by 10 January 1918** — the one thing it has always been correctly cited for.
2. **A fuller property description than the file records:** a *dwelling house with offices at the rear* on Main Street, Croom, **plus a strip of ground running down to the River Maigue** — part of a larger holding, with gardens. This is a substantial commercial premises, not a cottage.
3. **She redeemed the mortgage.** A widow in her eighties clearing a seven-year mortgage and taking the property in **fee simple** was not poor.
4. **The mortgage dates from 13 June 1911** — which sits alongside the note in `facts.md` that the 1911 indenture "may have funded the shop expansion." The 1911 census was taken 2 April 1911; the mortgage followed ten weeks later.

## ⚠️ Transcription reliability — the three disagree on every townland

| Field | base file | `-fs` | `-chat` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jeremiah's townland | Ballymacamore | Ballymacamore | Ballymac**anare** |
| The lands of… | **Skamerrygare** | **Samerrygare** | **Stanneryard** |
| …with the gardens of Croom and | **Shanacloon** | **Sharaction** | **Athenaclown** |
| Barony | *(omitted)* | Cosh**ina** | Coshma |

The forms differ wildly, which is the signature of automated reads rather than careful transcription. **Coshma** is correct (the real Co. Limerick barony containing Croom). The two land-names should be resolved against the actual memorial image before any of them is cited — they are probably Croom-parish townlands and may be searchable in Griffith's, but **no version should be trusted as it stands.**

**Do not quote the townland names from these files.** Everything else — parties, dates, the mortgage of 13 June 1911, the bank witnesses, the absence of any relationship — is consistent across all three and can be relied on.
