# Thomas Higgins (1833) and Bridget Brereton — Jersey City branch

**Compiled:** 2026-05-28 (updated 2026-05-29 with records scraped from Mary Anne's Ancestry tree, and the 1871 Parthia manifest transcribed by Matt)
**Sources:** Mary Anne Higgins (Australia-branch researcher) — DNA / Wicklow paper (Mar 2022); Mary Knight 1930 death email thread (Oct–Nov 2024); Irish Times reply thread (Nov 2024); Mary Anne's Ancestry tree records scrape (May 2026); 1871 Parthia passenger-list transcription (`incoming/parthia-list-1871.md`).
**Status:** Most of the previously circumstantial claims are now backed by primary-record indexes — Christopher's 1856 Rathmines baptism, the 1855 marriage, the 1865 NSW deposit, the 1871 Parthia manifest, the 1880 Jersey City census, the 1883 Jersey City directory, the 1882 Jersey City death, and the 1872 Henry Jerom baptism. The full Ireland-born children roster is now confirmed independently in two records (1871 manifest and 1880 census), and the family's Jersey City parish is identified.

---

## Who he was

Thomas Higgins — Laurence Higgins's paternal uncle and Matt's 3rd great-uncle. Second son of **Christopher Higgins** (1803) + Bridget. Born just after his older sister Mary Anne (1830, the future Australia emigrant). Following the Higgins family pattern, he stayed in Wicklow / Dublin long enough to marry.

**Baptism: 3 Jan 1833, Rathfarnham, Dublin** (Ireland Catholic Parish Registers 1655–1915; parish variants Crumlin / Rathfarnham and Crumlin, Diocese of Dublin). Father recorded as "Christr Higgins", mother as **"Bridget Higgins"** — see flag below. Sponsors: **James Burk(e?)** and **Anne Brady**.

**Flag: mother's name on the baptism.** The record lists his mother as **Bridget Higgins**, not Bridget Darcy (which is how Mary Anne identifies Christopher 1803's wife). Two readings:
- The priest used her married name instead of maiden name (not uncommon in Irish parish hands, but it does happen).
- Her maiden name was actually Higgins (possible — Christopher 1803 may have married into another Higgins family, or "Higgins" was used loosely).

Sponsors James Burk(e?) and Anne Brady are new names — potentially relatives of either parent and worth chasing for context.

The 1880 Jersey City census records Thomas's age as 55 (implying b. ~1825), but the 1833 baptism is the firm record; the census figure is an estimate.

## Bridget Brereton (a.k.a. Mary)

Thomas's wife. **Married Thomas Higgins on 18 November 1855 at Rathmines, Dublin city** (Ireland Catholic Parish Registers 1655–1915 — bride recorded in Latin as "Brigida Brereton"). The same Rathmines parish where Laurence later married Mary Verner (1873) and Mary Knight (1883) — three generations of Higgins ceremonies at one chapel.

She went by **two given names** across records:

- Her emigration manifest, marriage record, and death record give **Bridget**.
- The 1880 U.S. Census records her as **Mary** (independently corroborates the wife-name swap previously known only from the 1880 census record itself).
- Her 1872 son Henry Jerom's NJ baptism record gives her as **Bridget Bryarton**.

In index transcriptions her surname is variously rendered **Brereton / Bryarton / Bryerdon** — all phonetic hits on the same name.

**Death: 7 October 1882, Jersey City, NJ, age 48, married** (NJ Deaths and Burials Index 1798–1971, FHL Film 589833). Born ~1834 per the death record — consistent with her age 17 at the 1855 wedding, and roughly consistent with her 1880 census age of 48 (b. ~1832). She died only **eleven years after emigration**, leaving Thomas a widower with seven living children (eldest Christopher then 26). Likely buried at **St. Peter's Catholic Cemetery, Jersey City** (the family's known parish; see Jersey City section below) — or possibly Holy Name Cemetery, where infant Lawrence (1890) was reburied beside Mary Knight (1930).

## The Australia plan that didn't happen (now documented)

Thomas's sister **Mary Anne Higgins** (1830) had emigrated to NSW and married **John Woodward** around 1859–1860. Woodward offered to sponsor and finance Thomas's family passage. Previously Mary Anne's claim rested on family tradition; it is now backed by primary records:

- **12 July 1865 — Immigration Deposit lodged** (NSW Immigration Deposit Journals 1853–1900). Sponsor: **John Woodward**. Thomas listed at age 32, place of origin Dublin. Household members named: **Thomas, Bridget, Christopher, James, John** — five people, three sons.
- **1868 — Deposit refunded** (same source). The family never sailed.

Why they changed course is still unknown, but the three-year delay between deposit and refund is suggestive: something held the decision in limbo, not a snap reversal. They then sat in Ireland a further three years before sailing for the USA in 1871.

**Genealogical bonus:** the 1865 household roster names two previously undocumented sons (James and John) alongside the known Christopher (1856).

## The 1871 emigration

Thomas + Bridget Brereton arrived **New York on 3 August 1871** aboard the **R.M.S. Parthia** (William Watson, master; burthen 2279 tons; manifest sworn at NY 8 Aug 1871). Source: NY Passenger and Crew Lists 1820–1957; transcription of original manifest image in `incoming/parthia-list-1871.md` (Matt, May 2026).

The manifest is explicit: passengers were **taken on board at Liverpool**. The Higgins family is in the Liverpool-boarded section, not the Queenstown-boarded section. (The Ancestry index showed both ports because the Parthia ran the standard Cunard Liverpool → Queenstown → NY route and called at both.) **This supports Mary Anne's "lived briefly in Liverpool en route" hypothesis** — they boarded at Liverpool, not Queenstown.

Thomas listed: age 36, **Mechanic**, country "England," intended inhabitant U.S.A., steerage. Bridget listed: age 36, "Wife," same. The "English" nationality tag is consistent with a Liverpool boarding rather than a direct Irish departure.

**Resolved: the manifest ages are systematically fare-shaved by 2–3 years.** Christopher's Rathmines baptism (21 Sep 1856) independently fixes his birth year, and the manifest underreports him by 3 years; the same direction-and-magnitude pattern holds for Michael and (by census evidence) the others. **Mary Anne's birth years are well-grounded; the manifest is not a corrective source for absolute ages.**

**Resolved: William and Thomas Jr. are twins** — the 1880 Jersey City census carries the annotation "Twins" against their entries, lining up with the "two infants in a row" oddity on the manifest. The earlier hedge ("William (?)") comes off.

**Family count reconciliation:** The 1871 manifest shows **7 living Ireland-born children at emigration** (Christopher, James, John, Michael, Mary, Thomas Jr., William). With Henry Jerom born in Jersey City 30 Sep 1872, total documented = **8 children**. Mary Anne's earlier "9 total" figure is probably one off — or there was an 8th Ireland-born child who died before 1871 and isn't on any record we've found.

## Jersey City — anchor records

They settled in **Jersey City, NJ** — the same city across the Hudson from Manhattan where, fifteen years later, Thomas's nephew Laurence would set up his own American household.

### Parish: St. Peter's Catholic, Jersey City

Identified by **Henry Jerom Higgins's 30 Sep 1872 baptism record**, which gives both birthplace and christening place as **Saint Peter-Catholic, Jersey City**. This is the family's known Jersey City parish. The same St. Peter's complex (or its associated cemetery) is where **infant Lawrence Higgins's 1890 death record originally placed his burial** — a connection now bridged by a documented family link: Laurence's uncle Thomas's family had been parishioners there for at least 18 years before Lawrence was born.

### 1883 Jersey City Directory

Christopher Higgins, Roofer, **275 Grand Street** — still at the family address three years after the census, now in a different trade. Locates him in Jersey City four years before the 1887 Manhattan wedding witness role.

### Bridget's death — 7 Oct 1882

(See Bridget section above.) Thomas's widower trail thins after 1882 — Mary Anne hasn't located him in 1900 or later, and his death date and place are still open. Likely burial site for Bridget is **St. Peter's Catholic Cemetery, Jersey City** (family parish), with **Holy Name Cemetery, Jersey City** as the alternative — the latter is where infant Lawrence (1890) was reburied and where Mary Knight (1930) was laid in plot I I 7-8. **If Bridget and/or Thomas share either site with the Lawrence/Mary Knight burial line, the burial-record question becomes the strongest lever for tying the branches.**

### The Henry Jerom puzzle — "Charles" father field + missing in 1880

Henry Jerom's parish baptism record gives his **father as "Charles Higgins"**, not Thomas. Two possibilities:

1. **Indexer / scribe error.** "Thos." mis-read as "Chas." is a common 19th-century handwriting confusion on parish hands. The mother's name (Bridget Bryarton = Brereton) and the parish (St. Peter's, Jersey City — the documented family parish) and the date (14 months after the Parthia arrival) all align with Thomas + Bridget's family. Random coincidence — *a different* Higgins man with *a different* Bridget Brereton in the same parish — would be extraordinary.
2. **Different family.** A Charles Higgins married a different Bridget Brereton in the same parish around the same time. Possible but strained.

**Worth pulling the original baptism image** to resolve — if the priest's hand reads "Thos." it's almost certainly our family.

**The 1880 absence is the other open question.** Henry Jerom would have been 7 at the 1880 census but is not listed at 275 Grand Street. Most likely interpretation: **he died young between 1872 and 1880**, which would explain both the absence and Mary Anne's not having heard of him from family memory. Worth searching NJ death records 1872–1880 and **St. Peter's Catholic Cemetery, Jersey City** for a Henry Higgins child burial.

## Christopher Higgins (b. 1856, Rathmines) — the 1887 witness

Thomas + Bridget's eldest, following the Irish naming tradition (eldest son named for paternal grandfather Christopher 1803). **Baptized 21 Sep 1856 at Rathmines, Dublin** (Ireland Catholic Parish Registers 1655–1915; parents Thomas Higgins + Brigida Breton). Mary Anne identifies him as the "Christopher Higgins" who witnessed Laurence + Mary Knight's 6 Feb 1887 Manhattan ceremony — making him **Laurence's first cousin**, both grandsons of Christopher 1803.

The case is now well-supported:

- **Age:** Christopher was 31 in 1887 — appropriate witness age (baptism record settles it; manifest's age 12 in 1871 was fare-shaved).
- **Location:** Christopher was at **275 Grand Street, Jersey City** in 1880 (census, Coach driver) and **still at 275 Grand in 1883** (Jersey City directory, Roofer). Manhattan is a 12-mile ferry trip from there — entirely plausible for a wedding witness.
- **Family tie:** documented eldest son of Thomas (1833), Laurence's uncle.
- **Naming tradition:** eldest son carrying grandfather's name — standard Irish Catholic pattern.

Foundation has moved from circumstantial to multi-point documented.

## Open thread: 1890 Lawrence burial — with new parish context

In her Mary Knight 1930 death email, Mary Anne speculates that the rearrangement of infant Lawrence Higgins's 1890 burial from **St. Peter's Cemetery** (recorded on death cert) to **Holy Name Cemetery, Jersey City** (next to Mary Knight, decades later) may have been arranged by **either Margaret Higgins Marshall (Laurence's eldest daughter) or his uncle Thomas Higgins**. The implication: Thomas may have been alive in 1890–1930 to handle posthumous family arrangements, or his American descendants were keeping the plot.

**New context (May 2026):** St. Peter's Catholic is now confirmed as the **Thomas Higgins family's Jersey City parish** (per Henry Jerom's 1872 baptism there). So when infant Lawrence's death certificate originally placed him at St. Peter's Cemetery in 1890, that wasn't a random choice — it was the same parish Thomas's family had been part of since at least 1872, and probably since arrival in 1871. The burial-arrangement question now has a direct family-parish answer: someone in the Thomas branch (Thomas Sr. or one of his adult Jersey City sons) plausibly handled the original 1890 placement.

## Why this matters to our line

The 1871 → 1885ish gap between Thomas's emigration and Laurence's own crossing is suggestive. Laurence's choice of the Jersey City / Newark corridor — and the **fifteen-year lag** between Thomas's 1871 arrival and Laurence's mid-1880s crossing — looks like a younger nephew following an established family beachhead. If correct, it gives us a documented "chain migration" pattern: Mary Anne (1830) to Australia in the late 1850s; Thomas (1833) almost to Australia, then to Jersey City in 1871; Laurence (1854) to Jersey City / Newark around 1885; Edward (1873) to Montreal later; William's son Christopher A.W. (1885) to Brooklyn in 1908. Three generations of Higgins emigrants, each one likely informed by the one before.
