# Thomas Higgins (1833) and Bridget Brereton — Jersey City branch

**Compiled:** 2026-05-28
**Sources:** Mary Anne Higgins (Australia-branch researcher) — DNA / Wicklow paper (Mar 2022); Mary Knight 1930 death email thread (Oct–Nov 2024); Irish Times reply thread (Nov 2024)
**Status:** Synthesis of researcher correspondence. Few primary records cited; most claims rest on Mary Anne's reasoning + DNA on one son's line.

---

## Who he was

Thomas Higgins (b. 3 Jan 1833, Rathfarnham) — Laurence Higgins's paternal uncle and Matt's 3rd great-uncle. Second son of **Christopher Higgins** (1803) + **Bridget Darcy**, born just after his older sister Mary Anne (1830, the future Australia emigrant). Following the Higgins family pattern, he stayed in Wicklow / Dublin long enough to marry.

## Bridget Brereton (a.k.a. Mary)

Thomas's wife. The Brereton marriage itself isn't transcribed in the docs Mary Anne shared — likely a Dublin or Wicklow ceremony in the early-to-mid 1850s. Notable wrinkle: she went by **two given names** across records:

- Her emigration manifest and death record give **Bridget**.
- The 1880 U.S. Census records her as **Mary**.

Whether this reflects a deliberate name change in America, family preference, or just enumerator error, the surviving record doesn't say.

## The Australia plan that didn't happen

Mary Anne is clear that Thomas was originally bound for Australia. His sister **Mary Anne Higgins** (1830) had emigrated to NSW and married **John Woodward** around 1859–1860. Woodward offered to **sponsor and finance Thomas and his family's passage** to New South Wales. For reasons Mary Anne can't reconstruct, Thomas changed course.

## The 1871 emigration

Instead, Thomas + Bridget Brereton sailed for the **USA in 1871** with **eight children**. The passenger manifest contains a documented error: the family is listed as coming from **England**, not Ireland. Mary Anne's read is that they likely lived briefly in **Liverpool en route** — a few months perhaps — which got transcribed as the origin. The pattern isn't unusual; Mary Anne has seen it in other Irish-to-America emigration records.

## Jersey City

They settled in **Jersey City, NJ** — the same city across the Hudson from Manhattan where, fifteen years later, Thomas's nephew Laurence would set up his own American household. They appear in the **1880 U.S. Census** in Jersey City (the firmest record). By 1880 Bridget was going by **Mary** in census enumeration. After 1880 the trail thins; Mary Anne hasn't located them in 1900 or later.

## Nine children, one daughter, eight sons

The ninth (and last) was born in the USA after their arrival. Mary Anne has only been able to research **one** of the sons in depth:

- **Michael Higgins (1865–1924)** — his descendants are documented DNA matches to Mary Anne.
- **Deborah Thompson (née Higgins)** — US-based, descends from the Thomas 1833 branch; Mary Anne's 4th cousin.

The remaining six sons and the one daughter are a research dead end — Mary Anne describes it as "absolutely no luck."

## Christopher Higgins (b. 1856, Rathmines) — the 1887 witness

Thomas + Bridget's eldest, following the Irish naming tradition (eldest son named for paternal grandfather Christopher 1803). Mary Anne identifies him as the "Christopher Higgins" who witnessed Laurence + Mary Knight's 6 Feb 1887 Manhattan ceremony — making him **Laurence's first cousin**, both grandsons of Christopher 1803.

The logic chain: Thomas's family had been in Jersey City for fifteen years by 1887; Laurence had been corresponding with his uncle Thomas from Dublin (which means Laurence and his daughter Margaret would have known there was a Higgins contact in America when they crossed). Christopher 1856 was 31 in 1887 — the right age to stand as a wedding witness.

**The identification rests on naming tradition + geographic plausibility — no baptismal record for Christopher 1856 has been cited.** Foundation is circumstantial.

## Open thread: 1890 Lawrence burial

In her Mary Knight 1930 death email, Mary Anne speculates that the rearrangement of infant Lawrence Higgins's 1890 burial from St. Peter's Cemetery (recorded on death cert) to **Holy Name Cemetery, Jersey City** (next to Mary Knight, decades later) may have been arranged by **either Margaret Higgins Marshall (Laurence's eldest daughter) or his uncle Thomas Higgins**. The implication: Thomas may have been alive in 1890–1930 to handle posthumous family arrangements, or his American descendants were keeping the plot.

## What we don't know

- Thomas's death date or place (presumably Jersey City sometime after 1880)
- Bridget Brereton's death date and place
- Identity of seven of their nine children
- Whether they had any contact with Laurence after Christopher 1856 served as witness in 1887
- Any documents from the Brereton side of Bridget's family
- The Brereton marriage record itself

## Why this matters to our line

The 1871 → 1885ish gap between Thomas's emigration and Laurence's own crossing is suggestive. Laurence's choice of the Jersey City / Newark corridor — and the **fifteen-year lag** between Thomas's 1871 arrival and Laurence's mid-1880s crossing — looks like a younger nephew following an established family beachhead. If correct, it gives us a documented "chain migration" pattern: Mary Anne (1830) to Australia in the late 1850s; Thomas (1833) almost to Australia, then to Jersey City in 1871; Laurence (1854) to Jersey City / Newark around 1885; Edward (1873) to Montreal later; William's son Christopher A.W. (1885) to Brooklyn in 1908. Three generations of Higgins emigrants, each one likely informed by the one before.
