# ⚠️ There are TWO Margaret Molonys at Croagh — the "unique candidate" argument is dead

**Raised:** 2026-08-20, while enumerating Michael Moloney's children at Ballymackeamore (thread 4a).
**Status:** **OPEN QUESTION, deliberately not resolved.** Evidence pulls both ways. A decisive test is proposed in §5.

---

## 1. The problem

`research/findings/patrick-gleeson/facts.md` identifies our Margaret as the daughter of **James Molony and Catherine Kenny**, baptised **31 December 1836** at Croagh. One of the load-bearing supports for that identification is stated there as:

> *"the 31 Dec 1836 Margaret is the **unique** Margaret Molony baptized in Croagh in the window (re-confirmed via FamilySearch's parent-index)"*

**That is false.** There is a second one:

| # | Baptism | Father | Mother | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **31 Dec 1836**, Croagh | **James Molony** | **Catherine Kenny** | current identification |
| **B** | **21 Oct 1840**, Croagh (Kilfinny) | **Michl Moloney** | **Hanora Moinehan** *(= Honora Minihan)* | Ancestry coll. 61039, record 2561506, microfilm 02420/03 — read at detail level 2026-08-20 |

Candidate **B** is a **sister of Jeremiah Molony of Ballymackeamore** (bapt. 28 Oct 1842 to the same couple) and of **James Molony** (bapt. 8 Oct 1847 — the 1874 groom) and **Bridget** (bapt. 27 Aug 1845).

⚠️ This is the **fourth** same-name collision found in a single day, and the first to strike at a conclusion this central.

## 2. The case for **A** — James Molony × Catherine Kenny (the status quo)

**The Irish naming convention fits candidate A almost perfectly.** Patrick and Margaret's children, in order:

| Child | Convention | Predicted from A | Fits? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Catherine** (1862, 1st daughter) | maternal grandmother | **Catherine Kenny** | ✅ |
| **James** (1863, 1st son) | paternal grandfather | James Gleeson | ✅ |
| **Honora** (1866, 2nd daughter) | paternal grandmother | Honora O'Brien | ✅ |

Three predictions, three hits, in order.

**Under B this breaks.** If Margaret were Michael × Honora Minihan's daughter, both grandmothers would be named **Honora** (Honora Minihan and Honora O'Brien) — so the **first** daughter should be Honora. She is not; she is the second, and the first is Catherine, a name with no obvious source in family B.

**The age-shaving pattern also favours A.** Margaret's documented understatement runs 9–10 years:

| Record | Stated | Implied birth | Shave if **A** (b.1836) | Shave if **B** (b.1840) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1901 census | 55 | 1846 | **10 yrs** ✅ | 6 yrs |
| 1911 census | 66 | 1845 | **9 yrs** ✅ | 5 yrs |
| 1922 death | 74 | 1848 | 12 yrs | 8 yrs |

The 1901 and 1911 figures land exactly on the documented 9–10 year pattern under A.

## 3. The case for **B** — Michael Moloney × Honora Minihan of Ballymackeamore

**B explains, in one stroke, four things that A leaves unexplained.**

| Event | Under **A** | Under **B** |
|---|---|---|
| **1881** — Patrick stands godfather to the son of **James Moloney** (b.1847, Michael's son) | an unexplained tie to a Croom merchant family | **Patrick godfathering his wife's brother's son** — ordinary brother-in-law behaviour |
| **1918** — **Jeremiah Molony of Ballymackeamore** releases a mortgage to **Margaret Gleeson, widow** | a distant cousin, degree unproven and probably unprovable | **a brother releasing a mortgage to his widowed sister** |
| **1944** — **Helen Ryan** (Jeremiah's granddaughter) is informant and executor on **Catherine Hayes's** death certificate, listed as *"Cousin"* | ~2nd cousin once removed — a stretch for someone who buried her | **first cousin once removed** — a natural fit |
| **1860** — the marriage witness **"Michaele Moloney"** | brother Michael (b.1840), aged 19 | **her father Michael** |

That is a lot of explanatory power. The **three-generation, two-continent Ballymackeamore relationship** — 1881 → 1918 → 1944 — becomes trivial under B and remains a genuine puzzle under A.

## 3a. ⚠️ The farmer→shopkeeper pattern weakens B's strongest leg

Raised by Matt, 2026-08-20, and it holds up. Look at the occupations across this whole circle:

| Person | Occupation |
|---|---|
| **Patrick Gleeson** | Farmer 1859–**1879** → **Shopkeeper 1881** → Carrier 1897 |
| **Margaret Gleeson** | **Shop Keeper**, Main Street Croom, 1901 and 1911 |
| **James Moloney** of Ballymackeamore | **Farmer**, 1874 |
| **James Moloney** of Croom | **Shopkeeper**, 1881 |
| Michael Moloney · Jeremiah Molony · James Gleeson of Scagh · Timothy Gleeson sr & jr · Mark Storin · David Quaid | Farmer, all |
| John Hayes · Peter Hayes | Weaver |

**Two men in this circle made the same Farmer → Shopkeeper move in the same two-year window, in the same village.** Patrick's pivot is bounded to **29 Nov 1879 – 3 Nov 1881**. James Moloney was a farmer in June 1874 and a Croom shopkeeper by February 1881.

Two consequences, and both cut against reading too much into the 1881 godparenthood:

**(a) Occupation is a poor discriminator here.** A trade change across a few years is normal in this group, not remarkable — so it is evidence neither for nor against any identification. It should stop being used either way.

**(b) The 1881 godparenthood may be a business tie, not a kin tie.** Patrick Gleeson and James Moloney were **both newly-minted shopkeepers in Croom village at the same moment**, and one stood godfather to the other's son in February 1881. Among tradesmen setting up in a small village, that is at least as legible as commerce and neighbourhood as it is as kinship.

**That removes the force of candidate B's first leg.** The 1881 sponsorship was listed above as something B explains and A does not — but A explains it perfectly well too, as one Croom shopkeeper standing for another's child. **B is left with three legs, not four**, and the two strongest (1918, 1944) both involve *Jeremiah's* line rather than Margaret's own generation.

## 4. Honest assessment

**The two lines of evidence are of different kinds, and neither is decisive.**

- **A rests on naming and age patterns** — statistical regularities that hold most of the time. Three-for-three in order is a strong signal, but conventions get broken, and a child can be named for an aunt, a godparent, or a favourite.
- **B rests on documented social behaviour across sixty-three years** — godparenthood, a mortgage, and a deathbed. Those are acts, not conventions. But every one of them is also fully compatible with a *cousin* relationship, which the 1860 consanguinity dispensation independently tells us existed *somewhere*.

**The dispensation cuts both ways and must not be used carelessly.** Patrick and Margaret were second cousins once removed, sharing an ancestral couple born c.1710–1750 **whose surname is unknown**. That means a real blood tie between Patrick and *some* Moloney line already exists by construction — so a warm relationship with the Ballymackeamore Molonys does **not** require Margaret to have been born into them.

⚠️ **Do not resolve this on the strength of how neatly B tidies the story.** Three identifications were overturned today by exactly that instinct — a hypothesis that explained things well, adopted before the disambiguating record was pulled.

## 5. ⭐ The decisive test

**Find out what became of candidate B — Margaret Moloney, b. 21 Oct 1840, daughter of Michael of Ballymackeamore.**

If she married someone else, or died young, or appears in a census after 1860 as anyone other than Margaret Gleeson, **she is eliminated and A stands unshaken**. This is a clean, findable, falsifying test rather than an accumulation of plausibility.

Where to look:
1. **Croagh/Kilfinny marriage register, 1858–1875** — it survives from 9 Jan 1844, so unlike her parents' generation her marriage *would* be in it.
2. **Civil marriage index from 1864** — a Margaret Molony marrying in Rathkeale or Croom districts, father Michael.
3. **The 1901/1911 censuses** — a Margaret of the right age at Ballymackeamore or wherever she settled.
4. **Civil deaths from 1871.**

**Second test, independent:** re-read the **1918 Registry of Deeds memorial** (transcriptions at `assets/external/transcripts-memories-gleeson-molony-1918.md` and `...-fs.md`). Deeds sometimes state the relationship between parties outright. If it calls Jeremiah her brother, that settles it for B; if it calls him a cousin or nothing at all, A survives.

**Third:** Jeremiah's own children's baptisms and the 1901 census for Ballymackeamore would show whether the family used the name Margaret again — families rarely reuse a living sibling's name.
