## The critical question: where was our Patrick in 1901?

We have ONE non-parish record of Patrick (1864 Petty Sessions, Scaugh/Croom). We don't have any death record. We don't know if he was alive in 1901, where he lived, or what happened after Jane's 1878 baptism.

If Patrick was alive in 1901 at age 70 and still farming in Scagh, the **1901 Census of Ireland** for Croom parish should show him. That's the single most decisive test.

## Most likely interpretation

**Probably NOT our Patrick, but worth verifying** because:

1. **Birth year match is striking but weak alone** — Patrick Gleeson born ~1830 was a common profile across Munster
2. **James + Margaret children combo could be coincidence** — those are the most common Irish Catholic names of the era
3. **Killaloe location is geographically wrong** unless we have evidence of migration
4. **"Born Co Clare" is the strongest disqualifier** — our Patrick is presumed Limerick-born; the census recorded *his stated* birthplace

## Decisive tests

### Test 1: Eliminate the Killaloe candidate

Search the **1901 Irish Census** for any Gleeson household in Croom parish:
- URL: census.nationalarchives.ie/search
- Filter: Surname Gleeson (any spelling) + DED Croom (or Croom RD) + Age 65–75

| Outcome | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Patrick Gleeson found in Croom 1901 | A different Patrick lives in Croom → Killaloe one isn't us |
| No Patrick Gleeson in Croom 1901, but family present (e.g., widow Margaret, adult children) | Patrick died pre-1901 in Croom |
| No Gleeson household in Croom 1901 at all | Family may have moved or emigrated — Killaloe becomes more interesting |

### Test 2: Lock the Killaloe candidate as a different family

Check the **1911 census** for the same Killaloe household. If Patrick + Margaret still in Gortmaggy with same surname/children pattern, that's a coherent Clare family — definitely not ours.

## Caveats

- Family migration *did* happen, especially for landless laborers seeking farm work. If our Patrick lost his Croom situation post-1878 and moved north to Killaloe area, that's not strictly impossible. But unusual.
- 1901 census birthplace field reflects what the head of household stated. Could be inaccurate (people sometimes gave parish-of-residence rather than birthplace).
- A "Margaret Gleeson" wife in 1901 could either be Margaret Moloney (still alive at ~65 in 1901) OR a second Margaret if Patrick remarried.
