# St. Mary's (St. Mary Magdalen's) Academy, Silver City, NM — Institutional History

**Date:** 2026-06-10
**Why we care:** This is the Catholic boarding institution where **Howard Robert Higgins** and his cousin **Willis "Bill" Rosser** were boarded in the early 1930s (see silver-city-st-marys-foster-2026-06-10.md). This note captures the school's history to give that family chapter its proper setting.

**Primary source:** National Register of Historic Places **Inventory–Nomination Form, "Saint Mary's Academy Historic District"** — prepared by **Susan M. Berry, Assistant Director, Silver City Museum, August 1981** (NPS). *(Same Susan Berry who, decades later, identified St. Mary's as the boys' institution for this family.)* Address: **1813 N. Alabama St., Silver City, Grant County, NM.**

---

## Origins of parochial education in Grant County (Sisters of Mercy)

- **1881** — St. Vincent Parish, Silver City (then comprising all of Grant County), came under Rev. **Peter Bourgade** (later Bishop of Tucson, then Archbishop of Santa Fe).
- **1883** — Bourgade brought several **Sisters of Mercy** (originally from Ireland, lately from Mesilla, NM), who established a Motherhouse and opened the **Academy of Our Lady of Lourdes** in a three-storey downtown school building. This is the "legacy going back to the early 1880s" that makes the campus "the one tangible reminder of early parochial education in Grant County."
- **1887** — the Sisters opened a charity hospital → incorporated **1891 as St. Joseph's Hospital** → **1902 St. Joseph's Sanatorium** (tuberculosis treatment; Silver City was a noted health resort for "consumptives").
- The Sisters of Mercy ran school + hospital until **1915**.

## The Sisters of St. Joseph take over (1915)

- **1915** — Silver City was transferred from the **Tucson Diocese** to the newly created **Diocese of El Paso** (62,394 sq mi, incl. southern NM). *(This is why 1930s sacramental/diocesan records run through El Paso → today's Diocese of Las Cruces.)*
- The Sisters of Mercy sold their holdings to newly arrived **Sisters of St. Joseph from Tipton, Indiana**, led by **Sister Mary Magdalen Thomas**, and returned to Tucson.
- **August 1915** — Sister Mary Magdalen and four others incorporated as the **Sisters of St. Joseph of Silver City**.

## Founding of St. Mary Magdalen's Academy (1918–19)

- **March 1918** — the Sisters bought the **Lee residence** (a 1906 Mission-style adobe built by wealthy tubercular patient Aubrey F. Lee of Baltimore, d. 1908). **St. Mary Magdalen's Academy opened in the Lee house in early 1918.**
- **Dec 1918** — purchased the neighboring **Jackson house** (an imposing 1910 brick mansion), which became the **girls' dormitory**. Monsignor **William Wright** (Spiritual Director) financed land purchases; the Sisters took on heavy mortgage debt.
- Billed first as a **"select boarding school for young ladies and girls."**
- **1919 — the Academy began accepting BOYS ages 5 to 12**, after a **boys' dormitory** was built (builder Phil Hornbuckle). Also added that year: a dining room for 75 pupils (in the former Jackson servants' quarters) and a steam-heating plant. The campus had a garden, poultry yard, and dairy that supplied most of its food.
- A **1922 brochure, "A Trip Through St. Mary's Academy,"** describes luxurious dormitories, a study hall, library, and electrical fittings; the letterhead advertised "Private Rooms," "Spacious Campus and Tennis Courts," and "Special Training for Backward Pupils."

## The 1922 fire and rebuilding

- **Dec 16, 1922 (early morning)** — the **Lee house**, where the Sisters and most students lived, was **destroyed by fire**; the school library and two pianos were lost. Loss was insured.
- **Fall 1923** — a brick **chapel-and-convent building** (T-shaped) was built on the burned site by El Paso contractor **J.E. Morgan** (~$9,000). A grander $40,000 administration building, designed by **Trost & Trost** of El Paso, was announced but **never built**. Wings were added to the boys' dormitory in 1923.
- **1930** — an auditorium building was moved onto campus from the mining camp of **Santa Rita, NM**.

## Amalgamation with Concordia, Kansas (1926–28) — the record trail

- The Sisters of St. Joseph of Silver City stayed small (a 1923 group of Irish postulants briefly raised them to 21, but 8 left within a year) and carried ~$93,000 of debt.
- **1925** — Mother Magdalen Thomas wrote to the **Sisters of St. Joseph of Concordia, Kansas**, seeking amalgamation for survival; Concordia assumed the debt.
- **Feb 24, 1926** — the first Concordia Sisters arrived; **Sister Innocentia Brennan** became Superior.
- **March 10, 1928** — the St. Mary's Academy property was **officially deeded to the Concordia community.**
- ⇒ **During Howard and Bill's stay (~1931–34), the institution was run by the Concordia Sisters of St. Joseph** — confirming that early-1930s enrollment/boarding records belong at the **Concordia (Nazareth Motherhouse) archives**, not the defunct Silver City community.

## Decline and closure

- The **Academy operated until 1965**, then became **St. Mary's Inter-Parochial School** (a private day school).
- **Closed in 1980** (financially unsustainable). The campus became **"St. Mary's Center,"** and by 1981 a **birthing center** was being organized in the former boys' dormitory.
- The ~24-acre campus (≈7 acres nominated) was placed on the **National Register of Historic Places** in 1982; buildings include the Jackson house, priest's house, 1923 chapel/convent, 1919 boys' dorm, music building, heating plant, and more.

## Relevance to our family (quick read)

- Howard (b. 1927) and Bill (b. 1923) fit the **boys' program (ages 5–12)** that began in **1919**; they'd have lived in the **1919 boys' dormitory**.
- The school was a genuine **boarding institution** with on-site dorms, dining, and farm — consistent with the family lore that the boys "lived there and were in school" and "complained of being hungry."
- Records custodian for their era = **Concordia Sisters of St. Joseph** (deeded 1928) → reinforces the outreach plan in research/outreach/silver-city-records-inquiries-2026-06-10.md.
