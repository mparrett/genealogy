## The marriage I'm looking for

- **Bride:** Margaret **Moloney** (one L). Almost certainly from **Croagh / Kilfinny parish, Co. Limerick** (Diocese of Limerick) — see natal-family section below.
- **Most likely date:** **~1860–1861** (see dating logic below). Range to search: **1856–1862**.
- **Most likely location (in priority order):** Croagh/Kilfinny (bride's natal parish) → Croom → Hospital (groom's natal parish) → Adare and the Croom–Kilfinny border parishes.

## Why ~1860–1861 (revised from the older "late 1850s" estimate)

Their **firstborn was Catherine**, baptized **31 January 1862 at Croom** (FamilySearch-indexed, found 2026-06-08; *"Catharina Gleeson, father Patritii Gleeson, mother Margæ Molony"*). A firstborn in Jan 1862 implies a marriage roughly **9–18 months earlier, i.e. mid-1860 to mid-1861**. Their second child James (our direct ancestor) was baptized 23 Dec 1863. Patrick is already documented at Scagh as a **Farmer** by 11 Sept 1859 (a single man, presumably, since no children predate Catherine). So the realistic window is **1860–1861, with 1856–1859 as a tail** in case there was an earlier marriage and infant losses we haven't found.

## Why this record matters so much

1. **It would name Margaret's father.** We have a strong but *inferential* identification of her parents as **James Molony + Catherine Kenny** of Croagh (from a 31 Dec 1836 Croagh baptism), but no record yet directly names our Margaret's father. The marriage record names both fathers and locks it.
2. **It would give both spouses' ages** — resolving two live conflicts:
   - **Margaret's age** is contested. The Croagh baptism puts her birth at **1836**, but she self-reported **age 55 in 1901 (→1846)** and **66 in 1911 (→1845)**. We currently reconcile this with a ~9–10-year "age-shaving" pattern, but a marriage-record age would be a clean independent check.
   - **Patrick's birth year** is a three-way open question — **1829 vs 1833** (two Hospital baptisms to the same parents) **vs ~1837** (his 1897 death cert gives age 60). A stated age at marriage would help triangulate.
3. **The witnesses are likely siblings.** Irish marriage witnesses are very often a sibling of each spouse. Given our Moloney sibling cluster (below), a witness named Moloney would corroborate the whole natal-family reconstruction.
